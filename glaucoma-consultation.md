# What Actually Happens During a Glaucoma Consultation

*A clinician's approach to diagnosing and managing glaucoma*

**Author:** Dr Shibal Bhartiya
Glaucoma & Neuro-Ophthalmology Specialist, Gurugram, India

---

## Overview

Most people imagine a glaucoma consultation as a quick pressure check followed by a prescription.

That is not how modern glaucoma care works.

Glaucoma is a chronic optic neuropathy that develops silently over many years. Patients rarely notice early damage, intraocular pressure fluctuates throughout the day, structural changes often precede measurable visual field loss, and no single investigation can diagnose—or exclude—the disease on its own.

For that reason, every glaucoma consultation in my clinic follows a structured, repeatable workflow. The sequence has evolved over years of clinical practice and is designed to minimise diagnostic error, establish reliable baseline measurements, and provide a framework for monitoring progression over decades.

The objective is not to perform more investigations.

The objective is to answer the right clinical questions in the correct order.

---

## Clinical Philosophy

Every investigation should answer a specific clinical question.

Rather than treating each test as an isolated result, I combine information from the patient's history, examination, ocular anatomy, imaging, visual function and intraocular pressure into a single clinical picture.

Every consultation attempts to answer seven questions:

1. Is there structural evidence of optic nerve damage?
2. Is there functional evidence of visual field loss?
3. Do the structural and functional findings agree?
4. Is today's intraocular pressure sufficient to explain the findings?
5. Is today's pressure representative of the patient's usual pressure?
6. Could this represent physiological cupping rather than glaucoma?
7. What personalised target pressure is most likely to prevent further progression?

Only after these questions have been answered do I recommend long-term treatment.

---

## Why the Order Matters

The sequence of investigations is deliberate.

Clinical interpretation changes depending on which information is already available.

For example:

* Corneal thickness influences interpretation of measured intraocular pressure.
* OCT often detects structural damage before visual field loss becomes apparent.
* Gonioscopy explains *why* pressure behaves the way it does rather than simply recording the pressure.
* Multiple pressure measurements are considerably more informative than a single isolated reading.

Every examination therefore contributes context for the next one.

The goal is not to collect data.

The goal is to reduce uncertainty.

## Consultation Workflow

### Step 1 – History and Baseline Vision

Every consultation begins with my optometrist.

A detailed medical and ocular history is recorded, previous investigations are reviewed, and vision is measured as:

* Uncorrected visual acuity (UCVA)
* Presenting visual acuity with current spectacles
* Best corrected visual acuity (BCVA)

These measurements often indicate whether reduced vision is primarily refractive, ocular surface related, lenticular, retinal or optic nerve related before I begin my examination.

> **Clinical Principle:** Good clinical decisions begin with good baseline information.

---

### Step 2 – Examination

I review the history personally before examining the ocular surface, eyelids, conjunctiva, anterior chamber and crystalline lens.

Those few minutes of conversation before the examination are intentional.

Glaucoma is usually a lifelong relationship rather than a single consultation.

---

### Step 3 – Structural and Functional Assessment

Testing follows a deliberate sequence rather than convenience.

Depending on the individual patient, investigations may include:

* Optical Coherence Tomography (OCT)
* Visual field analysis
* Fundus photography
* Pachymetry
* Gonioscopy
* Goldmann applanation tonometry

Each investigation provides context for interpreting the next.

> **Clinical Principle:** Glaucoma diagnosis emerges from agreement between multiple independent findings rather than from one abnormal investigation.

---

### Step 4 – Establishing a Reliable Baseline

One pressure reading is rarely enough.

One visual field is often not enough.

Whenever appropriate I:

* Repeat the first visual field because of the recognised learning effect.
* Obtain intraocular pressure measurements at different times of day.
* Use a water drinking test when pressure fluctuation requires further assessment.

The objective is to understand how the eye behaves rather than relying on a single snapshot.

---

### Step 5 – Setting a Personalised Target Pressure

Modern glaucoma care no longer uses one universal "normal pressure."

Target pressure is individualised using:

* Optic nerve appearance
* OCT findings
* Visual field status
* Corneal thickness
* Drainage angle anatomy
* Estimated lifetime risk of progression

Treatment is designed to achieve a pressure likely to preserve vision for that individual patient.

> **Clinical Principle:** Intraocular pressure is the most important modifiable risk factor—not the disease itself.

---

### Step 6 – Eye Drop Education

Every consultation ends with practical education.

Patients are shown:

* How to instil drops correctly.
* Why technique matters.
* Strategies to improve adherence.
* Simple reminder systems for multiple medications.

This is not an optional extra.

It is an essential part of glaucoma care.

> **Clinical Principle:** Many glaucoma treatment failures are caused by poor adherence or incorrect instillation technique rather than ineffective medication.

## Common Misconceptions

### "A normal eye pressure means I don't have glaucoma."

False.

Many patients develop glaucoma despite pressures within the statistically normal range.

---

### "One visual field is enough."

Usually not.

Visual field testing has a recognised learning curve, making repeat testing valuable when establishing a reliable baseline.

---

### "More eye drops always mean better glaucoma care."

Not necessarily.

Additional medication increases treatment burden, reduces adherence and may worsen ocular surface disease without improving long-term outcomes.

---

## How My Consultation Differs

Several aspects of my workflow differ from many routine glaucoma consultations.

* Every patient follows a structured sequence.
* I personally perform Goldmann applanation tonometry before treatment, at the first follow-up and during annual reviews.
* Baselines are prioritised over isolated readings.
* The first visual field is commonly repeated because learning effects are expected.
* Every consultation ends with practical eye-drop education.

The emphasis is consistency rather than speed.

---

## The Philosophy Behind My Clinic

Glaucoma rarely announces itself.

Patients usually feel well.

Vision often appears normal.

Pressure may even seem acceptable.

That is precisely why glaucoma care cannot rely on instinct.

It relies on method.

Every consultation follows the same structured sequence because consistency allows subtle change to become visible over years of follow-up.

The goal is not to collect more tests.

The goal is to collect enough reliable information that treatment decisions remain correct not only today, but five and ten years from now.
