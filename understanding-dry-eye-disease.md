# Understanding Dry Eye Disease

> **Dry eye disease is one of the most common eye conditions worldwide, yet it is also one of the most misunderstood.**

Many people think dry eye simply means that the eyes do not produce enough tears.

In reality, dry eye is usually a chronic condition involving the tear film, eyelids, meibomian glands, ocular surface, nerves and inflammation.

Fortunately, most people can achieve significant improvement with the right diagnosis, treatment and long-term management.

---

# What Is Dry Eye Disease?

Dry eye disease occurs when the tear film is unable to adequately protect and lubricate the surface of the eye.

This may happen because:

- the eyes do not produce enough tears
- tears evaporate too quickly
- the quality of tears is poor
- inflammation damages the ocular surface
- several of these problems occur together

Dry eye is not simply a lack of tears.

It is a disease of the entire ocular surface.

---

# What Does the Tear Film Do?

A healthy tear film:

- Keeps the eye comfortable
- Provides a smooth optical surface for clear vision
- Nourishes the cornea
- Protects against infection
- Washes away dust and debris
- Helps maintain healthy eye tissues

Even small disturbances in the tear film can affect vision and comfort.

---

# Common Symptoms

Dry eye symptoms vary considerably.

You may experience:

- Burning
- Stinging
- Grittiness
- Foreign body sensation
- Redness
- Watering
- Blurred vision
- Fluctuating vision
- Eye fatigue
- Heavy eyelids
- Light sensitivity
- Difficulty reading
- Difficulty using digital screens
- Contact lens intolerance

Symptoms often fluctuate throughout the day.

---

# What Causes Dry Eye?

Dry eye is usually multifactorial.

Common causes include:

- Ageing
- Meibomian gland dysfunction
- Prolonged screen use
- Reduced blinking
- Hormonal changes
- Contact lenses
- Certain medications
- Autoimmune diseases
- Previous eye surgery
- Environmental factors

Many patients have more than one contributing factor.

---

# Meibomian Gland Dysfunction

The meibomian glands produce the oily layer of the tear film.

If these glands become blocked or unhealthy:

- tears evaporate faster
- the tear film becomes unstable
- dry eye symptoms worsen

For many patients, treating the eyelids is just as important as using lubricating eye drops.

---

# Why Does Vision Fluctuate?

Many people notice that vision becomes clearer immediately after blinking.

This happens because blinking briefly restores a smooth tear film.

As the tears evaporate, vision may become blurred again.

This is one of the classic signs of dry eye disease.

---

# Why Do Dry Eyes Water?

Many patients are surprised when watery eyes are diagnosed as dry eye.

When the eye becomes irritated, it produces reflex tears.

These tears do not contain enough oil and evaporate quickly.

As a result, watering and dryness often occur together.

---

# How Is Dry Eye Diagnosed?

Diagnosis involves much more than asking about symptoms.

An eye examination may include:

- Assessment of the tear film
- Eyelid examination
- Meibomian gland evaluation
- Tear break-up time
- Ocular surface staining
- Tear production tests
- Assessment for inflammation
- Evaluation of contributing conditions

No single test diagnoses every patient.

The entire clinical picture is important.

---

# Treatment

Treatment depends on the underlying cause.

Options may include:

- Artificial tears
- Lubricating gels
- Warm compresses
- Lid hygiene
- Meibomian gland treatment
- Prescription anti-inflammatory drops
- Nutritional advice
- Environmental modifications
- Treatment of associated eyelid disease

Many patients benefit from combining several approaches.

---

# Lifestyle Changes

Simple daily habits often improve symptoms.

These include:

- Blinking consciously during screen use
- Following the 20-20-20 rule
- Staying hydrated
- Using a humidifier when appropriate
- Avoiding direct air conditioning
- Wearing sunglasses outdoors
- Getting adequate sleep

---

# Can Dry Eye Be Cured?

Dry eye is usually a chronic condition.

Most patients improve significantly with appropriate treatment.

The goal is long-term control rather than a permanent cure.

Treatment plans often need adjustment over time.

---

# When Should You See an Ophthalmologist?

Arrange an eye examination if you experience:

- Persistent burning
- Redness
- Fluctuating vision
- Excessive watering
- Light sensitivity
- Pain
- Difficulty reading
- Poor tolerance of contact lenses
- Symptoms that interfere with daily life

Early treatment often prevents symptoms from becoming more severe.

---

# My Philosophy

Dry eye treatment is about much more than prescribing artificial tears.

Successful management requires understanding why the ocular surface has become unhealthy.

Every patient deserves:

- an accurate diagnosis
- an individualised treatment plan
- education about their condition
- realistic expectations
- long-term follow-up when needed

My goal is not simply to reduce symptoms today.

It is to restore comfort, improve vision and protect the health of the ocular surface over time.

---

# Frequently Asked Questions

## Is dry eye permanent?

It is often a long-term condition, but symptoms can usually be controlled very effectively.

---

## Are artificial tears enough?

Some patients need only lubricants.

Others require treatment for inflammation, meibomian gland dysfunction or associated eyelid disease.

---

## Does screen use cause dry eye?

Prolonged screen use reduces blink rate and often worsens symptoms.

---

## Why do my eyes water if they are dry?

Reflex tearing is a common response to ocular surface irritation.

---

## Can dry eye affect vision?

Yes.

An unstable tear film can cause fluctuating or blurred vision.

---

# Key Take-Home Messages

- Dry eye is a disease of the entire ocular surface—not simply a lack of tears.
- Symptoms vary widely and often fluctuate.
- Watering eyes can be a sign of dry eye.
- Good treatment begins with an accurate diagnosis.
- Long-term management is often more important than a single eye drop.
- Most patients can achieve significant improvement with an individualised treatment plan.
