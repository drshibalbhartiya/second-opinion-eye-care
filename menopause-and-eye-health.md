# Menopause and Eye Health

> **Menopause affects far more than hot flushes and bone health. It also changes the eyes.**

Many women notice that their eyes become drier, more sensitive, or that their vision seems different around the time of menopause.

These changes are common.

Some are temporary.

Others require treatment and regular monitoring.

Understanding these changes helps women protect both their comfort and their long-term vision.

---

# Why Does Menopause Affect the Eyes?

The eyes contain receptors for several hormones, including:

- Estrogen
- Progesterone
- Androgens

As hormone levels change during menopause, they influence:

- Tear production
- Tear quality
- Meibomian gland function
- Corneal sensitivity
- Ocular surface health
- Lens metabolism

These changes explain why many women experience eye symptoms during and after menopause.

---

# Common Eye Problems During Menopause

Many women experience one or more of the following:

- Dry eyes
- Burning or stinging
- Grittiness
- Excessive watering
- Blurred vision
- Light sensitivity
- Contact lens intolerance
- Fluctuating vision
- Eye fatigue
- Difficulty reading

Fortunately, most symptoms can be improved with appropriate treatment.

---

# Dry Eye Disease

Dry eye is one of the most common eye conditions affecting menopausal women.

Hormonal changes may reduce both the quantity and quality of tears.

The tear film becomes less stable, leading to irritation and inflammation.

Symptoms include:

- Burning
- Stinging
- Foreign body sensation
- Blurred vision
- Watering
- Redness
- Tired eyes

Many women are surprised that excessive watering may actually be a sign of dry eye.

---

# Why Do Dry Eyes Become Worse?

Several factors often occur together:

- Reduced tear production
- Meibomian gland dysfunction
- Increased evaporation
- Air conditioning
- Digital device use
- Certain medications
- Autoimmune diseases
- Contact lens wear

Most women have more than one contributing factor.

---

# Meibomian Gland Dysfunction

The meibomian glands produce the oily layer of the tear film.

Hormonal changes may reduce their function.

Without enough oil:

- Tears evaporate more quickly.
- Dry eye becomes more severe.
- Vision fluctuates.

Treating the eyelids is often as important as treating the tears.

---

# Changes in Vision

Some women notice:

- Difficulty reading
- More glare
- Frequent changes in spectacle prescription
- Intermittent blurred vision

Sometimes these symptoms are caused by dry eye.

Sometimes they reflect normal ageing changes in the lens.

A comprehensive eye examination can identify the cause.

---

# Contact Lens Intolerance

Women who have comfortably worn contact lenses for years may suddenly develop:

- Dryness
- Irritation
- Reduced wearing time

Improving the ocular surface often restores comfort.

Sometimes changing the lens material or wearing schedule helps.

---

# Cataracts

Age remains the biggest risk factor for cataracts.

Since menopause usually occurs during the years when cataracts begin to develop, many women notice increasing glare, reduced contrast and blurred vision.

Fortunately, cataract surgery is highly successful when appropriate.

---

# Glaucoma

Glaucoma becomes more common with increasing age.

Women should not assume that vision changes are simply due to menopause.

Regular comprehensive eye examinations remain essential.

Early glaucoma often causes no symptoms.

---

# Macular Degeneration

The risk of age-related macular degeneration also increases with age.

Regular retinal examinations become increasingly important after the age of 50.

---

# Does Hormone Replacement Therapy Affect the Eyes?

Hormone replacement therapy (HRT) has complex effects.

Some studies suggest it may improve certain dry eye symptoms in some women.

Other studies show little benefit or mixed results.

HRT should never be started solely to improve eye symptoms.

Decisions regarding hormone therapy should always be made with a woman's primary physician or gynaecologist.

# Lifestyle Habits That Help

Simple daily habits make a significant difference.

These include:

- Staying well hydrated
- Blinking regularly
- Limiting prolonged screen time without breaks
- Following the 20-20-20 rule
- Wearing sunglasses outdoors
- Sleeping adequately
- Exercising regularly
- Not smoking

---

# Managing Dry Eye

Treatment depends on severity.

Options may include:

- Artificial tears
- Lubricating gels
- Warm compresses
- Lid hygiene
- Meibomian gland treatment
- Prescription anti-inflammatory eye drops
- Dietary modifications
- Environmental changes

Treatment should always be individualised.

---

# Everyday Tips

If your eyes feel dry:

- Blink more often during screen use.
- Increase humidity where possible.
- Avoid direct air conditioning.
- Stay hydrated.
- Wear wraparound sunglasses outdoors.
- Remove eye makeup carefully.
- Follow your treatment plan consistently.

---

# When Should You See an Eye Doctor?

Arrange a comprehensive eye examination if you experience:

- Persistent dry eyes
- Blurred vision
- Eye pain
- Redness
- Flashes of light
- Sudden floaters
- Loss of vision
- Difficulty driving at night
- Frequent spectacle prescription changes

Do not assume these symptoms are "just menopause."

---

# My Philosophy

Women's eye health deserves the same attention as heart health, bone health and mental wellbeing during menopause.

Many eye symptoms can be treated successfully.

Early diagnosis often prevents years of unnecessary discomfort.

Good eye care during menopause combines:

- Regular eye examinations
- Healthy lifestyle habits
- Appropriate treatment
- Individualised care
- Patient education

The goal is not simply comfortable eyes today.

The goal is preserving healthy vision for decades to come.

---

# Frequently Asked Questions

## Does menopause cause dry eyes?

It commonly contributes to dry eye because hormonal changes affect tear production and tear quality.

---

## Can menopause change my glasses prescription?

Yes.

Some women notice temporary or permanent changes in vision.

---

## Should I stop wearing contact lenses?

Not necessarily.

Many women continue wearing them comfortably with appropriate management.

---

## Will drinking more water cure dry eye?

Hydration supports overall health but is usually only one part of treatment.

---

## Should every menopausal woman have an eye examination?

Yes.

Regular comprehensive eye examinations become increasingly important with age.

---

# Key Take-Home Messages

- Hormonal changes affect the eyes.
- Dry eye is extremely common during menopause.
- Most symptoms can be improved with treatment.
- Good nutrition supports healthy eyes.
- Regular eye examinations remain essential.
- Do not ignore changes in vision.
- Early treatment improves comfort and protects long-term vision.
