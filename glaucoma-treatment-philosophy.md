# My Glaucoma Treatment Philosophy

> *Glaucoma is not simply a disease of eye pressure. It is a disease of long-term decisions.*

Every patient who walks into my clinic has a unique story, different priorities, and a different risk of losing vision.

My job is not simply to prescribe eye drops, perform surgery, or lower eye pressure.

My responsibility is to help people make the right decisions—at the right time—for the rest of their lives.

This document explains how I think about glaucoma care, what I do, what I don't do, and who is likely to benefit from seeing me.

---

# My Philosophy

I believe glaucoma care should be:

- Evidence-based
- Individualised
- Honest
- Conservative when possible
- Decisive when necessary
- Built around the patient's life—not the disease

The best glaucoma specialist is not the one who performs the most surgery.

The best glaucoma specialist is the one who helps patients keep useful vision with the least possible burden throughout their lifetime.

Sometimes that means treatment.

Sometimes it means changing treatment.

Sometimes it means surgery.

Sometimes it means doing absolutely nothing except careful observation.

Knowing the difference is where experience matters.

---

# What We Are Really Treating

We are not treating:

- an eye pressure number
- an OCT printout
- a visual field defect
- a diagnosis on paper

We are treating the risk of future vision loss.

That distinction changes everything.

Many patients with elevated eye pressure never develop glaucoma.

Some patients with "normal" eye pressure do.

Some scans look frightening but remain stable for years.

Others appear reassuring while subtle progression continues.

Every decision requires context.

---

# What I Optimise For

Every recommendation I make is guided by one question:

> **What gives this patient the greatest chance of preserving useful vision for the rest of their life while creating the least possible disruption to their life?**

That means balancing:

- risk of progression
- current vision
- age
- life expectancy
- rate of progression
- quality of life
- occupation
- hobbies
- medication burden
- financial considerations
- likelihood of adherence
- patient preferences

Lower eye pressure is important.

But lower eye pressure is not the final goal.

The final goal is lifelong visual function.

---

# What I Measure That Many Clinics Don't

Medicine often measures numbers.

Eye pressure.

Visual fields.

OCT scans.

Cup-disc ratio.

Number of medications.

These are important.

But they are not the whole story.

Some of the most important clinical outcomes are rarely documented.

---

## Time Spent With the Patient is a Clinical Outcome

A consultation is not successful because it was quick.

It is successful because the patient leaves understanding:

- what they have
- why they are being treated
- what to expect
- how treatment works
- what warning signs matter
- what the long-term plan is

If that conversation takes longer, it is not inefficiency.

It is treatment.

Understanding improves adherence.

Understanding reduces anxiety.

Understanding improves long-term outcomes.

Time is a clinical intervention.

---

## Teaching Eye Drop Technique is a Clinical Outcome

Many patients receive glaucoma medication without ever being shown how to use it properly.

Poor technique leads to:

- medication waste
- poor disease control
- unnecessary side effects
- contamination
- frustration
- incorrect assumptions that treatment has failed

Every patient should know how to:

- instil a single drop
- avoid touching the bottle tip
- perform punctal occlusion when appropriate
- space multiple medications correctly
- build a routine that can be maintained for decades

Teaching this is not an optional extra.

It is glaucoma treatment.

A patient using one medication correctly is often better treated than a patient using three medications incorrectly.

---

## Confidence is a Clinical Outcome

Many patients arrive frightened.

Some have been told they will inevitably go blind.

Others have spent months searching the internet.

Some have received conflicting opinions.

My aim is not only to treat glaucoma.

It is to replace uncertainty with understanding.

A well-informed patient is better equipped to make good decisions over a lifetime.

---

# What I Do

## I Listen

Before examining your eyes, I want to understand:

- your concerns
- your work
- your daily routine
- your goals
- your previous experiences
- what matters most to you

Good glaucoma care begins with listening.

---

## I Confirm the Diagnosis

Not every patient labelled as having glaucoma actually has glaucoma.

Some have:

- physiological cupping
- ocular hypertension
- tilted discs
- myopia-related optic nerve changes
- imaging artefacts

Treatment should begin only after the diagnosis is clear.

---

## I Look for Progression

The single most important question in glaucoma is not:

*"Do you have glaucoma?"*

It is:

*"Is it progressing?"*

Progression—not a single test result—drives treatment decisions.

---

## I Use Evidence

Recommendations are guided by:

- clinical examination
- optic nerve assessment
- OCT
- visual fields
- corneal thickness
- eye pressure
- risk factors
- published evidence
- clinical judgement

No single test decides treatment.

Patterns do.

---

## I Individualise Treatment

Two patients with identical scans may receive different recommendations.

Because patients are different.

Age matters.

Occupation matters.

Life expectancy matters.

Lifestyle matters.

Risk tolerance matters.

Medicine should never become an algorithm.

---

## I Prefer the Simplest Effective Treatment

Every additional medication increases:

- cost
- side effects
- complexity
- chances of non-adherence

More treatment is not better treatment.

Appropriate treatment is better treatment.

---

## I Believe in Shared Decision-Making

Patients deserve to understand:

- why treatment is recommended
- what alternatives exist
- what happens if they choose observation
- what surgery can achieve
- what surgery cannot achieve

The final decision should be informed—not imposed.

---

# What I Don't Do

I do not treat numbers without context.

I do not recommend surgery simply because surgery is possible.

I do not prescribe additional medications unless they are likely to make a meaningful difference.

I do not promise perfect vision.

I do not promise that glaucoma can be cured.

I do not use fear to improve compliance.

I do not believe every patient needs the newest technology.

I do not believe every patient needs the most expensive treatment.

I do not rush consultations simply to increase volume.

I do not dismiss patient concerns because their scans appear "normal."

I do not believe that more intervention automatically means better care.

---

# My Approach to Surgery

Surgery is an important tool.

It is not the destination.

When surgery offers the best chance of preserving vision while reducing treatment burden, I recommend it.

When it does not, I do not.

Every surgical recommendation balances:

- expected benefit
- expected risk
- patient priorities
- long-term quality of life

---

# Who Should See Me

You may benefit from seeing me if:

- you have been diagnosed with glaucoma
- someone suspects you may have glaucoma
- your eye pressure is elevated
- your optic nerve appears suspicious
- your OCT or visual field results are confusing
- your glaucoma seems to be worsening despite treatment
- you are considering laser treatment
- you are considering glaucoma surgery
- you want a second opinion
- you have a strong family history of glaucoma
- you have normal-tension glaucoma
- you have complex glaucoma with multiple previous treatments

---

# Who May Especially Benefit From a Second Opinion

A second opinion can be valuable if:

- your diagnosis is uncertain
- different doctors have given conflicting advice
- surgery has been recommended
- your scans and symptoms do not seem to match
- your glaucoma appears to be progressing despite treatment
- you simply want reassurance before making a major decision

Seeking another opinion is not a sign of distrust.

It is good medicine.

---

# What Happens During a Consultation

Every consultation is built around four questions:

1. Do you actually have glaucoma?
2. Is it progressing?
3. Does it need treatment now?
4. What is the least burdensome way to protect your vision over the long term?

Everything else follows from those questions.

---

# My Promise to Patients

I will tell you what I know.

I will tell you what I do not know.

I will explain why I recommend a treatment.

I will explain when observation is reasonable.

I will answer your questions honestly.

I will never recommend treatment simply because it is available.

I will recommend what I believe is in your best long-term interest.

---

# Working With Your Existing Eye Doctor

Most patients do not need to change ophthalmologists.

Many seek a second opinion simply to confirm a diagnosis or discuss treatment options.

I am happy to work alongside your existing eye doctor whenever appropriate.

The goal is always the same:

The best possible care for the patient.

---

# Final Thoughts

Glaucoma is a lifelong condition.

Good glaucoma care is not built on a single consultation, a single scan, or a single eye pressure reading.

It is built on thoughtful decisions, careful follow-up, honest conversations, and a partnership between doctor and patient that may last for decades.

My aim is simple:

**To help you preserve useful vision for the rest of your life while imposing the least possible burden on the life you want to live.**
