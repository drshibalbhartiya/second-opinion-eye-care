# Why Establishing a True Baseline IOP Is the Foundation of Glaucoma Care

Every treatment decision in glaucoma begins with one question:

> **What is this patient's true baseline intraocular pressure?**

Without a reliable baseline, it is impossible to know whether treatment is achieving the desired target pressure or whether the disease is progressing despite apparently satisfactory numbers.

## Not Every Pressure Measurement Is a Baseline

A single intraocular pressure recorded during one clinic visit is not a baseline.

Intraocular pressure fluctuates throughout the day and from one day to another.

Stress, posture, time of day, and normal biological variation all influence the measurement.

For this reason, I prefer to establish a baseline using **at least three Goldmann applanation tonometry measurements**, obtained on different days or at different times of the day.

Goldmann applanation tonometry remains the clinical reference standard.

While non-contact tonometry (air-puff tonometry) is useful for screening, it should not be used to establish a patient's baseline IOP or guide long-term glaucoma management.

## Looking Beyond the Clinic Pressure

Many patients spend only a few minutes in my clinic.

Glaucoma, however, exists for the remaining 23 hours and 55 minutes.

The highest pressure may occur long before or long after the clinic visit.

Understanding these fluctuations is often more informative than relying on a single measurement.

## The Role of the Water Drinking Test

Ideally, every patient would undergo a diurnal variation study.

In practice, this is not always feasible.

For many patients, I therefore perform a **baseline Water Drinking Test (WDT).**

The WDT is **not a diagnostic test for glaucoma.**

Its value lies elsewhere.

It serves as a practical surrogate for identifying pressure peaks and assessing how the eye responds to physiological stress.

This information helps me understand pressure behaviour that may not be apparent during routine office hours.

## Assessing Control After Treatment

Once treatment has begun, the objective is not simply to record another isolated IOP.

The objective is to determine whether pressure remains controlled throughout the day.

Depending on the clinical situation, I may repeat:

- Goldmann applanation tonometry at different times
- A Water Drinking Test
- A diurnal variation study

These investigations provide a much more meaningful assessment of treatment success than a single clinic measurement.

## When Standard Testing Is Not Enough

Occasionally, patients continue to show structural or functional progression despite apparently satisfactory office pressures and normal daytime diurnal variation.

In carefully selected cases, I have used **24-hour continuous intraocular pressure monitoring with the Triggerfish® contact lens sensor** to evaluate pressure-related patterns that conventional daytime testing may fail to detect.

Although not required for routine glaucoma care, continuous monitoring can provide valuable additional information in complex cases where unexplained progression persists.

## My Perspective

Glaucoma management is not based on a single pressure.

It is based on understanding a patient's pressure profile over time.

A carefully established baseline allows meaningful target pressure setting, rational treatment decisions, and more accurate interpretation of future change.

For me, establishing the baseline is not simply the first step in glaucoma treatment.

It is the foundation upon which every subsequent decision is built.
