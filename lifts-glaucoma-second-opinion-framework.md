# The L.I.F.T.S.™ Glaucoma Second Opinion Framework

## A Structured, Evidence-Based Approach to Personalised Glaucoma Care

Glaucoma is a lifelong disease, and the decisions made early in its course can influence vision for decades. Yet diagnosis and treatment are rarely straightforward. Variability in eye pressure, imaging results, and visual field testing can make it difficult to determine whether glaucoma is truly present, whether it is progressing, and whether treatment should be intensified.

The **L.I.F.T.S.™ Framework**, devised by **Dr Shibal Bhartiya**, provides a comprehensive, evidence-based evaluation to guide personalised glaucoma care and lifelong vision preservation.

The framework follows five structured steps:

- **L – Longitudinal Record Integration**
- **I – Independent Diagnostic Re-Evaluation**
- **F – Functional Rate-of-Progression Analysis**
- **T – Target IOP & Lifetime Risk Profiling**
- **S – Proportional & Sustainable Management Planning**

---

# Why Was the L.I.F.T.S.™ Framework Developed?

Many patients seeking a glaucoma second opinion have questions such as:

- Do I really have glaucoma?
- Has my glaucoma actually progressed?
- Is surgery necessary?
- Am I on the right medications?
- Is my eye pressure low enough?
- Why do different doctors recommend different treatments?

The answer often lies not in performing more tests, but in interpreting existing information more systematically.

The L.I.F.T.S.™ Framework was developed to provide a structured clinical pathway that integrates historical records, advanced diagnostics, and individual risk factors to support accurate, evidence-based decision making.

---

# L — Longitudinal Record Integration

## Establishing the True Baseline

### Clinical Purpose

**Synthesising historical investigations to distinguish genuine progression from testing variability.**

Glaucoma is a chronic disease that unfolds over years, not days. A single OCT scan or visual field rarely provides enough information to determine whether meaningful change has occurred.

The first step of the framework involves reviewing:

- Previous OCT RNFL and GCC scans
- Visual field reports
- Intraocular pressure measurements
- Central corneal thickness
- Optic disc photographs
- Previous treatment history

Instead of focusing on one isolated investigation, longitudinal analysis allows trends to emerge while reducing the influence of testing artefacts, learning effects, and machine variability.

The central question becomes:

> **What has changed over time—not simply what appears abnormal today?**

---

# I — Independent Diagnostic Re-Evaluation

## Confirming the Diagnosis

### Clinical Purpose

**Distinguishing true glaucoma from anatomical or physiological mimics.**

Not every large optic cup represents glaucoma.

Similarly, not every abnormal OCT scan indicates progressive optic nerve damage.

This stage includes careful reassessment of:

- Optic nerve head appearance
- Retinal nerve fibre layer
- Ganglion cell complex
- Gonioscopy
- Corneal biomechanics
- Clinical history

Potential alternative diagnoses include:

- Physiological cupping
- High myopia
- Tilted optic discs
- Optic nerve drusen
- Congenital disc anomalies
- Non-glaucomatous optic neuropathies

Confirming the diagnosis before escalating treatment helps avoid unnecessary medication, laser procedures, or surgery.

---

# F — Functional Rate-of-Progression Analysis

## Determining Disease Velocity

### Clinical Purpose

**Measuring how quickly glaucoma is changing rather than whether minor fluctuations exist.**

Not all glaucoma progresses at the same rate.

Some patients remain stable for years, while others lose vision rapidly despite apparently acceptable eye pressure.

This stage evaluates:

- Visual field trend analysis
- Event analysis
- OCT progression
- Functional deterioration
- Structure-function correlation

Patients may be classified as:

- Stable
- Slow progression
- Moderate progression
- Rapid progression

Understanding disease velocity allows treatment intensity to match actual clinical need.

---

# T — Target IOP & Lifetime Risk Profiling

## Calculating Your Personalised Safe Pressure

### Clinical Purpose

**Determining an individual target intraocular pressure based on lifetime risk.**

There is no universal "normal" eye pressure.

An eye pressure that is safe for one patient may be too high for another.

Target IOP is individualised using factors including:

- Current stage of glaucoma
- Rate of progression
- Baseline eye pressure
- Central corneal thickness
- Age
- Life expectancy
- Optic nerve status
- Fellow eye involvement
- Overall risk profile

The objective is not merely lowering eye pressure—it is preserving useful vision throughout life.

---

# S — Proportional & Sustainable Management Planning

## Matching Treatment to Risk

### Clinical Purpose

**Ensuring treatment intensity is appropriate for the patient's disease and quality of life.**

Effective glaucoma care balances:

- Disease severity
- Progression rate
- Medication burden
- Ocular surface health
- Quality of life
- Long-term sustainability

Management recommendations may include:

- Observation
- Medication optimisation
- Selective Laser Trabeculoplasty (SLT)
- Minimally Invasive Glaucoma Surgery (MIGS)
- Filtering surgery when indicated

Rather than automatically escalating treatment, the framework asks whether the current management strategy is proportionate to the patient's actual risk.

# Frequently Asked Questions

## Is the L.I.F.T.S.™ Framework only for glaucoma?

No. It can also be useful for glaucoma suspects, ocular hypertension, and patients in whom the diagnosis remains uncertain.

---

## Does everyone require every investigation?

No. Investigations are individualised based on your clinical findings, previous records, and treatment history.

---

## Can this framework prevent unnecessary surgery?

In some patients, careful reassessment may demonstrate that glaucoma is stable or progressing more slowly than initially suspected. In others, it may confirm that earlier intervention is appropriate. The goal is accurate decision making rather than avoiding or promoting surgery.

---

## Why is a second opinion useful in glaucoma?

Because glaucoma management often depends on interpreting multiple investigations over time. Reviewing historical records, disease progression, and individual risk factors may provide additional clarity when important treatment decisions are being considered.
