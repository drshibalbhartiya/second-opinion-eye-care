# Hormone Replacement Therapy (HRT) and Eye Health

> **Hormone replacement therapy can improve quality of life for many women during menopause, but its effects on the eyes are complex and sometimes misunderstood.**

Many women ask whether HRT helps dry eyes, protects vision, or increases the risk of eye disease.

The answer is not straightforward.

Research suggests that hormones influence many parts of the eye, but HRT is **not an eye treatment**, and its effects vary between individuals.

This guide explains what we currently know—and what we still don't know.

---

# What is Hormone Replacement Therapy?

Hormone Replacement Therapy (HRT) involves replacing some of the hormones that decline during menopause.

Depending on individual circumstances, treatment may include:

- Estrogen alone
- Estrogen with progesterone
- Different doses
- Different formulations
- Oral tablets
- Skin patches
- Gels
- Sprays
- Vaginal preparations

The choice depends on a woman's medical history, symptoms and overall health.

---

# Why Do Hormones Affect the Eyes?

The eyes are hormone-sensitive organs.

Hormone receptors have been identified in:

- The cornea
- The conjunctiva
- The lacrimal glands
- The meibomian glands
- The retina
- The lens
- The optic nerve

As hormone levels change during menopause, many women notice changes in vision or eye comfort.

These changes may improve, remain stable or persist despite HRT.

---

# Can HRT Improve Dry Eye?

Dry eye disease is one of the most common eye problems during and after menopause.

Because estrogen and androgens influence tear production and the function of the meibomian glands, researchers have studied whether HRT improves dry eye symptoms.

The results have been mixed.

Some women report:

- Less burning
- Better comfort
- Reduced irritation
- Improved tear stability

Others notice:

- No significant change
- Persistent symptoms despite treatment

Current evidence does **not** support prescribing HRT solely to treat dry eye disease.

Dry eye should be managed using established eye treatments, regardless of whether a woman is taking HRT.

---

# Why Are the Results Mixed?

Dry eye is rarely caused by one factor alone.

Several contributors often occur together:

- Hormonal changes
- Meibomian gland dysfunction
- Ageing
- Digital device use
- Air conditioning
- Autoimmune diseases
- Contact lens wear
- Certain medications

Because dry eye has many causes, replacing hormones alone may not resolve symptoms.

---

# Does HRT Affect Eye Pressure?

Research has explored whether estrogen influences intraocular pressure (IOP).

Some studies suggest that HRT may be associated with a small reduction in average eye pressure.

However:

- the effect is generally modest
- not every woman experiences it
- HRT should **never** be prescribed to lower eye pressure
- glaucoma treatment should never be replaced by HRT

Women with glaucoma should continue all prescribed glaucoma medications unless advised otherwise by their ophthalmologist.

---

# Can HRT Prevent Glaucoma?

At present, there is **no evidence** that HRT prevents glaucoma or replaces standard glaucoma treatment.

Some observational studies have suggested that longer lifetime exposure to estrogen may influence glaucoma risk.

However, the relationship is complex, and current evidence is insufficient to recommend HRT as a strategy to prevent glaucoma.

Regular comprehensive eye examinations remain the most important way to detect glaucoma early.

---

# Cataracts and HRT

Researchers have investigated whether HRT influences cataract development.

Results have been inconsistent.

Some studies suggest a possible protective effect for certain women.

Others have shown little or no difference.

Age remains the strongest risk factor for cataract formation.

---

# Age-Related Macular Degeneration (AMD)

The relationship between HRT and age-related macular degeneration remains uncertain.

Some studies suggest a possible reduction in risk.

Others have found no significant association.

At present, HRT is **not recommended** to prevent AMD.

The best ways to reduce the risk of AMD include:

- not smoking
- controlling blood pressure
- eating a healthy diet
- maintaining a healthy weight
- regular eye examinations

---

# Contact Lens Comfort

Some women notice that contact lenses become less comfortable during menopause.

If HRT improves ocular surface comfort, contact lens tolerance may also improve.

However, many women continue to require:

- lubricating eye drops
- improved lid hygiene
- changes in lens materials
- reduced wearing time

---

# Can Vision Change After Starting HRT?

Some women notice temporary fluctuations in vision after starting or changing hormone therapy.

Possible reasons include:

- changes in tear film quality
- altered corneal hydration
- changes in refractive measurements

Most changes are mild.

Persistent or significant vision changes should always be evaluated by an eye care professional.

---

# What About Diabetes and Blood Pressure?

Many women taking HRT also have conditions such as diabetes or hypertension.

These conditions may have a greater effect on long-term eye health than HRT itself.

Good control of:

- blood sugar
- blood pressure
- cholesterol

helps protect vision over time.

---

# Looking After Your Eyes During Menopause

Whether or not you take HRT, healthy habits matter.

These include:

- Regular comprehensive eye examinations
- Managing dry eye symptoms early
- Wearing sunglasses outdoors
- Eating a balanced diet
- Staying physically active
- Avoiding smoking
- Staying hydrated
- Following treatment plans consistently

Small daily habits often have a greater impact than any single medication.

---

# Should Every Woman Taking HRT Have an Eye Examination?

Yes.

Regular eye examinations become increasingly important after the age of 40–50 years.

They help detect:

- glaucoma
- cataracts
- retinal disease
- macular degeneration
- diabetic eye disease
- dry eye disease

Many of these conditions develop gradually and may not cause symptoms in their early stages.

---

# Myths About HRT and Eye Health

## "HRT cures dry eyes."

False.

Some women improve.

Many require additional dry eye treatment.

---

## "HRT prevents glaucoma."

False.

No current evidence supports this.

---

## "If I take HRT, I don't need eye examinations."

False.

Routine eye care remains essential.

---

## "Vision changes during menopause are always hormonal."

False.

Blurred vision may result from dry eye, cataracts, glaucoma, retinal disease, diabetes or other eye conditions.

Persistent symptoms should never be ignored.

---

# When Should You See an Ophthalmologist?

Arrange an eye examination if you experience:

- Persistent dry eyes
- Blurred vision
- Double vision
- Increasing glare
- Eye pain
- Sudden loss of vision
- New floaters
- Flashes of light
- Frequent prescription changes

Prompt evaluation allows many conditions to be treated before permanent vision loss occurs.

---

# My Philosophy

Hormone replacement therapy should be viewed as one part of a woman's overall health—not as a treatment for eye disease.

For some women, HRT may improve eye comfort.

For others, it may make little difference.

The best approach is individualised care based on:

- symptoms
- examination findings
- overall health
- personal preferences
- current scientific evidence

Eye care during menopause should focus on prevention, early diagnosis and maintaining long-term visual health.

# Frequently Asked Questions

## Does HRT improve dry eyes?

Some women notice improvement, but many continue to need conventional dry eye treatment.

---

## Can HRT lower eye pressure?

Some studies suggest a small reduction in average eye pressure, but the effect is usually modest and should not replace glaucoma treatment.

---

## Does HRT prevent glaucoma?

No.

Current evidence does not support HRT as a glaucoma prevention strategy.

---

## Can HRT improve blurry vision?

If blurred vision is caused by dry eye, improving the tear film may help.

However, blurred vision has many possible causes and should always be evaluated.

---

## Should I stop HRT if my eyes become dry?

Not necessarily.

Discuss your symptoms with both your ophthalmologist and the doctor managing your menopause care before making any changes.

---

# Key Take-Home Messages

- Hormones influence many parts of the eye.
- HRT is **not** an established treatment for eye disease.
- Some women experience improvement in dry eye symptoms, while others do not.
- Current evidence does not support using HRT to prevent glaucoma or macular degeneration.
- Healthy lifestyle habits and regular eye examinations remain the most effective ways to protect long-term vision.
- Decisions about HRT should be based on overall health, menopausal symptoms and individual risk—not eye health alone.
