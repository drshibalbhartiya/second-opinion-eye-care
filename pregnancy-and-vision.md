# Pregnancy and Vision

> **Pregnancy brings many changes to the body—including the eyes. Most are temporary, but some require prompt medical attention.**

Many women notice changes in their vision during pregnancy.

Some develop dry eyes.

Others notice blurred vision or difficulty wearing contact lenses.

Occasionally, changes in vision may signal an important medical condition that needs urgent evaluation.

Knowing which changes are expected—and which should never be ignored—can help protect both your vision and your overall health.

---

# Can Pregnancy Affect the Eyes?

Yes.

Hormonal, metabolic and circulatory changes during pregnancy can affect many parts of the eye.

Most changes are temporary and improve after delivery.

Some, however, require careful monitoring and treatment.

---

# Common Eye Changes During Pregnancy

Many women experience:

- Mild blurred vision
- Dry eyes
- Contact lens discomfort
- Increased light sensitivity
- Temporary changes in glasses prescription
- Eye fatigue

These symptoms are usually temporary.

---

# Dry Eyes During Pregnancy

Hormonal changes may reduce tear production and alter tear quality.

You may notice:

- Burning
- Grittiness
- Watering
- Redness
- Fluctuating vision
- Difficulty wearing contact lenses

Preservative-free artificial tears often help, but persistent symptoms should be assessed by an ophthalmologist.

---

# Can My Glasses Prescription Change?

Yes.

Fluid retention during pregnancy may temporarily alter the shape of the cornea.

This can lead to:

- Mild blurred vision
- Temporary prescription changes
- Difficulty adapting to contact lenses

Because these changes often improve after delivery, major changes to spectacle prescriptions are usually avoided unless absolutely necessary.

---

# Contact Lens Wear

Some women find contact lenses less comfortable during pregnancy.

This may be due to:

- Dry eye
- Mild corneal swelling
- Hormonal changes

Temporary use of glasses may improve comfort.

---

# Pregnancy and Glaucoma

Managing glaucoma during pregnancy requires careful planning.

Some glaucoma medications may not be appropriate during pregnancy or breastfeeding.

Treatment decisions should always balance:

- The mother's eye health
- The risk of glaucoma progression
- The baby's safety

Never stop glaucoma medications without discussing them with your ophthalmologist.

---

# Diabetes and Pregnancy

Women with diabetes should have regular eye examinations before, during and after pregnancy.

Pregnancy may accelerate **diabetic retinopathy** in some women.

Regular retinal examinations help detect changes early.

---

# High Blood Pressure and Pregnancy

One of the most important causes of visual symptoms during pregnancy is **pre-eclampsia**.

This condition can affect both mother and baby.

Eye symptoms may include:

- Blurred vision
- Flashing lights
- Temporary loss of vision
- Double vision
- Increased sensitivity to light

These symptoms require **urgent medical assessment.**

---

# When Should You Seek Immediate Medical Attention?

Contact your doctor immediately if you develop:

- Sudden loss of vision
- Persistent blurred vision
- Flashes of light
- A sudden increase in floaters
- A curtain or shadow over your vision
- Double vision
- Severe eye pain
- Severe headache with visual symptoms

These symptoms should never be dismissed as a normal part of pregnancy.

---

# Can Pregnancy Cause Permanent Vision Loss?

Most pregnancy-related eye changes are temporary.

However, vision loss caused by retinal disease, severe hypertension or other serious conditions may become permanent if treatment is delayed.

Prompt evaluation is essential.

---

# Is LASIK Recommended During Pregnancy?

No.

LASIK is generally postponed during pregnancy and while breastfeeding because hormonal changes may temporarily affect the shape of the cornea and the stability of your glasses prescription.

---

# Can I Use Eye Drops During Pregnancy?

Not all eye drops are equally suitable during pregnancy.

Some medications are considered relatively safe.

Others should be avoided unless clearly necessary.

Always tell your ophthalmologist if you are:

- Pregnant
- Planning pregnancy
- Breastfeeding

Never start or stop prescription eye medications without medical advice.

---

# Looking After Your Eyes During Pregnancy

Simple measures can help maintain eye comfort.

These include:

- Staying well hydrated
- Getting adequate rest
- Using preservative-free lubricating eye drops if recommended
- Managing diabetes and blood pressure carefully
- Attending scheduled eye examinations
- Reporting new visual symptoms promptly

---

# My Philosophy

Pregnancy is a time of enormous physiological change.

Most changes in vision are temporary.

Some are not.

The challenge is knowing the difference.

My goal is to reassure women when symptoms are expected, while recognising the rare situations where urgent treatment protects both vision and overall health.

Every pregnant woman deserves careful, individualised eye care.

---

# Frequently Asked Questions

## Is blurred vision normal during pregnancy?

Mild fluctuations may occur.

Persistent or sudden blurred vision should always be assessed.

---

## Can pregnancy make dry eyes worse?

Yes.

Hormonal changes commonly contribute to dry eye symptoms.

---

## Should I change my glasses during pregnancy?

Minor prescription changes are often temporary.

Unless vision is significantly affected, it may be reasonable to wait until after pregnancy before updating your glasses.

---

## Can pregnancy worsen glaucoma?

Pregnancy affects every woman differently.

Treatment should always be individualised in consultation with your ophthalmologist.

---

## When should I worry about vision changes?

Sudden vision loss, flashing lights, severe headaches, double vision or persistent blurred vision require prompt medical evaluation.

---

# Key Take-Home Messages

- Pregnancy can temporarily affect vision and eye comfort.
- Dry eye and contact lens intolerance are common.
- Glasses prescriptions may fluctuate during pregnancy.
- Women with diabetes or glaucoma require careful eye care.
- Sudden visual symptoms should never be ignored.
- Regular communication between your ophthalmologist and obstetrician helps ensure the safest care for both mother and baby.
