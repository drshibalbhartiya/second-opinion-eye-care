# The C.L.E.A.R.™ Dry Eye Second Opinion Framework

## A Structured, Evidence-Based Approach to Personalised Dry Eye Care

Dry eye disease is far more complex than simply having "dry eyes." It may result from inadequate tear production, excessive tear evaporation, inflammation, eyelid disease, nerve dysfunction, systemic illness, medications, or a combination of several factors. Because different causes require different treatments, identifying the underlying mechanism is essential before developing an effective management plan.

The **C.L.E.A.R.™ Framework**, devised by **Dr Shibal Bhartiya**, provides a comprehensive, evidence-based evaluation to identify the root cause of dry eye disease and guide personalised treatment.

The framework follows five structured steps:

- **C – Comprehensive Etiology Sub-Classification**
- **L – Lipid & Meibomian Gland Structure Mapping**
- **E – Environmental, Systemic & Iatrogenic Triaging**
- **A – Advanced Inflammation & Biomarker Profiling**
- **R – Root-Cause Targeted Rehabilitation**

---

# Why Was the C.L.E.A.R.™ Framework Developed?

Many patients continue to experience burning, watering, fluctuating vision, or eye discomfort despite using artificial tears for months or even years.

Often, the problem is not that treatment has failed—it is that the underlying cause has never been fully identified.

The C.L.E.A.R.™ Framework was developed to provide a systematic approach that evaluates every major contributor to dry eye disease before recommending treatment.

Rather than asking:

> **"Which eye drops should I use?"**

the framework first asks:

> **"Why are your eyes dry in the first place?"**

---

# C — Comprehensive Etiology Sub-Classification

## Determining the Primary Dry Eye Subtype

### Clinical Purpose

**Distinguishing evaporative, aqueous-deficient, and neurosensory dry eye disease.**

Dry eye is not a single condition.

Patients may have:

- Evaporative Dry Eye
- Aqueous-Deficient Dry Eye
- Mixed Mechanism Dry Eye
- Neurosensory Ocular Pain

Correctly identifying the primary mechanism allows treatment to target the underlying disease rather than simply masking symptoms.

---

# L — Lipid & Meibomian Gland Structure Mapping

## Evaluating Meibomian Gland Function

### Clinical Purpose

**Assessing gland architecture, tear film stability, and blinking function.**

More than 80% of patients with dry eye have some degree of Meibomian Gland Dysfunction (MGD).

This stage evaluates:

- Infrared meibography
- Meibomian gland morphology
- Meibum quality
- Non-invasive tear breakup time (NIBUT)
- Blink completeness
- Tear film lipid layer

Detecting gland dropout or obstruction early may allow treatment before permanent gland loss occurs.

---

# E — Environmental, Systemic & Iatrogenic Triaging

## Identifying External and Systemic Contributors

### Clinical Purpose

**Recognising medications, systemic disease, lifestyle factors, and preservative toxicity.**

Dry eye often reflects more than an eye problem.

The framework reviews:

- Autoimmune diseases
- Sjögren syndrome
- Thyroid eye disease
- Diabetes
- Rosacea
- Hormonal influences
- Antihistamines
- Antidepressants
- Blood pressure medications
- Chronic preserved eye drops
- Digital device use
- Workplace environment

Addressing these contributing factors often improves long-term outcomes.

---

# A — Advanced Inflammation & Biomarker Profiling

## Measuring Ocular Surface Health

### Clinical Purpose

**Evaluating inflammation, epithelial damage, and tear film homeostasis.**

Chronic inflammation is a major driver of dry eye disease.

Assessment may include:

- Fluorescein staining
- Lissamine green staining
- Tear osmolarity
- Matrix Metalloproteinase-9 (MMP-9)
- Corneal surface assessment

This stage helps determine disease severity and guides appropriate anti-inflammatory treatment.

---

# R — Root-Cause Targeted Rehabilitation

## Creating a Personalised Treatment Plan

### Clinical Purpose

**Replacing generic treatment with targeted therapy based on the underlying cause.**

Rather than recommending artificial tears alone, treatment is individualised according to each patient's diagnosis.

Management may include:

- Preservative-free lubricants
- Immunomodulatory therapy
- Thermal meibomian gland treatment
- Intense Pulsed Light (IPL)
- Lid hygiene
- Nutritional support
- Autologous serum tears
- Neurostimulation
- Scleral contact lens evaluation
- Lifestyle modification

The emphasis is always on treating the underlying disease process rather than simply relieving symptoms.

# Frequently Asked Questions

## Is dry eye always caused by reduced tear production?

No. Many patients produce enough tears but lose them too quickly because of Meibomian Gland Dysfunction or tear film instability.

---

## Why aren't artificial tears enough?

Artificial tears improve symptoms temporarily but do not treat inflammation, gland dysfunction, autoimmune disease, or nerve-related pain.

---

## Do I need every test?

No. Investigations are tailored according to your symptoms, examination findings, and previous treatment history.

---

## Can dry eye be cured?

Many forms of dry eye disease can be effectively managed with accurate diagnosis, targeted treatment, and long-term follow-up. The goal is to improve comfort, visual quality, and ocular surface health.
