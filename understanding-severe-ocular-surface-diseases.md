# Understanding Severe Ocular Surface Diseases

> **Some eye diseases do much more than cause dry eyes. They can damage the entire surface of the eye, affecting comfort, vision and quality of life for years or even a lifetime.**

Severe ocular surface diseases are uncommon, but they can have profound consequences if they are not recognised and treated early.

These conditions often require long-term care from ophthalmologists with expertise in ocular surface disease, dry eye and corneal disorders.

Although each condition has a different cause, many share similar eye complications and require similar treatment strategies.

---

# What Are Severe Ocular Surface Diseases?

The ocular surface includes:

- The cornea
- The conjunctiva
- The eyelids
- The tear film
- The meibomian glands
- The limbal stem cells

When these structures become severely inflamed or scarred, the eye may no longer be able to maintain a healthy, stable surface.

Patients may develop:

- Severe dry eye
- Chronic pain
- Light sensitivity
- Blurred vision
- Corneal damage
- Scarring
- Vision loss

---

# Which Conditions Can Cause Severe Ocular Surface Disease?

Some of the most important include:

- Ocular Graft-versus-Host Disease (GVHD)
- Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS)
- Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN)

Although uncommon, these conditions require lifelong monitoring.

---

# Ocular Graft-versus-Host Disease (GVHD)

GVHD may develop after a bone marrow or stem cell transplant.

The donor's immune cells attack the recipient's tissues, including the eyes.

Eye symptoms may include:

- Severe dryness
- Burning
- Grittiness
- Light sensitivity
- Blurred vision
- Excessive watering
- Difficulty opening the eyes
- Eye pain

The ocular surface often becomes chronically inflamed.

Without treatment, persistent inflammation may damage the cornea.

---

# Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS)

Stevens-Johnson Syndrome is a rare but serious immune reaction, often triggered by medications or infections.

It affects:

- Skin
- Mouth
- Airways
- Eyes

The eyes may become severely inflamed during the acute illness.

Long-term complications may continue for years after recovery.

---

# Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN)

TEN is a more extensive and severe form of the same disease spectrum as Stevens-Johnson Syndrome.

Patients often require intensive medical care.

Unfortunately, even after the skin heals, significant eye disease may remain.

---

# How Do These Diseases Affect the Eyes?

The inflammation may damage:

- Tear-producing glands
- Meibomian glands
- Eyelids
- Conjunctiva
- Cornea
- Limbal stem cells

This can lead to:

- Severe dry eye disease
- Persistent epithelial defects
- Corneal ulcers
- Corneal scarring
- Corneal blood vessel growth
- Eyelid scarring
- Eyelashes rubbing against the eye (trichiasis)
- Symblepharon (scar tissue joining the eyelid to the eyeball)
- Limbal stem cell deficiency

---

# Common Symptoms

Patients may experience:

- Constant dryness
- Severe burning
- Pain
- Foreign body sensation
- Redness
- Blurred vision
- Light sensitivity
- Difficulty reading
- Difficulty driving
- Reduced quality of life

Symptoms often fluctuate but rarely disappear completely without treatment.

---

# Why Early Treatment Matters

Inflammation causes damage over time.

Early treatment aims to:

- Reduce inflammation
- Preserve vision
- Protect the cornea
- Prevent scarring
- Improve comfort

Many patients require long-term follow-up.

---

# Treatment

Treatment depends on the underlying disease and its severity.

Most patients require a combination of therapies.

---

## Lubrication

Frequent preservative-free artificial tears help protect the ocular surface.

Lubricating gels and ointments may be useful at night.

---

## Serum Eye Drops

Some patients benefit from **autologous serum eye drops**, which are prepared from the patient's own blood.

Unlike artificial tears, serum eye drops contain naturally occurring growth factors, vitamins and proteins that support healing of the ocular surface.

They may help:

- Promote corneal healing
- Improve comfort
- Reduce persistent epithelial defects
- Support damaged ocular surface tissues

For selected patients with severe disease, serum eye drops can be an important part of treatment.

---

## Anti-inflammatory Treatment

Inflammation often requires treatment with medications prescribed by an ophthalmologist.

These may include:

- Topical anti-inflammatory eye drops
- Immunomodulatory therapy
- Short courses of corticosteroids when appropriate

Treatment is carefully individualised.

---

## Meibomian Gland Care

Many patients also develop meibomian gland dysfunction.

Warm compresses, eyelid hygiene and appropriate treatment may improve tear film stability.

---

## Scleral Contact Lenses

For many patients with severe ocular surface disease, **scleral contact lenses** can be life-changing.

Unlike conventional contact lenses, scleral lenses rest on the white part of the eye and vault over the cornea.

A reservoir of sterile fluid remains between the lens and the eye, continuously bathing the cornea.

Benefits may include:

- Reduced pain
- Improved comfort
- Better vision
- Protection of the corneal surface
- Reduced evaporation of tears

Not every patient is a candidate, but scleral lenses have transformed the quality of life of many people with severe ocular surface disease.

---

## Surgical Treatment

Some patients require surgery to manage complications.

Examples include:

- Removal of abnormal eyelashes
- Amniotic membrane transplantation
- Eyelid reconstruction
- Limbal stem cell transplantation
- Corneal transplantation in selected cases

Surgery is usually considered only after inflammation has been stabilised.

---

# Living With Severe Ocular Surface Disease

Successful management often involves:

- Regular follow-up
- Consistent treatment
- Protecting the ocular surface
- Avoiding environmental triggers
- Working closely with your ophthalmologist

Although these conditions are chronic, many patients achieve significant improvement with modern treatment.

---

# My Philosophy

Severe ocular surface disease affects much more than the eyes.

It affects reading, working, driving, relationships and quality of life.

The goal is not simply to prescribe eye drops.

The goal is to restore a healthy ocular surface, relieve pain, preserve vision and help patients return to daily life with as much comfort and independence as possible.

This requires patience, partnership and long-term care.

---

# Frequently Asked Questions

## Can these diseases be cured?

Most are chronic conditions.

Treatment aims to control inflammation, improve comfort and preserve vision.

---

## Are artificial tears enough?

Usually not.

Many patients require additional therapies such as serum eye drops, anti-inflammatory treatment or scleral contact lenses.

---

## What are serum eye drops?

They are lubricating eye drops prepared from your own blood and contain natural healing factors that support the ocular surface.

---

## What are scleral lenses?

They are specialised contact lenses that protect the cornea while providing continuous lubrication and often improving vision.

---

## Will I need lifelong follow-up?

Many patients benefit from long-term specialist care because these conditions can change over time.

---

# Key Take-Home Messages

- Severe ocular surface diseases can threaten both comfort and vision.
- GVHD, Stevens-Johnson Syndrome and Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis may all cause chronic ocular surface damage.
- Early diagnosis and long-term care are essential.
- Modern treatments—including preservative-free lubricants, serum eye drops and scleral contact lenses—have significantly improved outcomes for many patients.
- Every treatment plan should be individualised according to the severity of disease and the patient's needs.
