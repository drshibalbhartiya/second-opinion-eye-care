# Something Went Into My Eye: Do You Need to See a Doctor?

This is one of the most common situations.

Dust, sand, or a small particle enters the eye—and immediately causes discomfort.

The question is:
👉 will it settle, or does it need attention?

---

## What you may feel

- A constant “something in the eye” sensation  
- Watering  
- Redness  
- Irritation with blinking  

---

## What usually happens

In many cases, the eye tries to clear the particle on its own.

Blinking and natural tearing help remove small debris.

---

## What you can do safely

- Blink several times  
- Rinse gently with clean water  
- Avoid touching or rubbing the eye  

---

## What not to do

- Do not rub the eye (this can cause a scratch)  
- Do not try to remove deeply embedded particles yourself  
- Do not use unprescribed drops  

---

## When you should seek help

- The sensation does not go away  
- Pain increases  
- Vision becomes blurred  
- The eye remains red or sensitive  

---

## Why this matters

Sometimes the particle is gone—but it has left behind:
👉 a small surface scratch (corneal abrasion)

That is what causes ongoing discomfort.

---

## The core idea

**If the feeling persists, it is no longer about the particle—it is about the effect it caused.**
