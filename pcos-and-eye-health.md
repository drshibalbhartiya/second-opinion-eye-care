# PCOS and Eye Health

> **Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is best known for affecting hormones, periods and fertility—but it can also influence eye health.**

Most women with PCOS will never develop serious eye disease because of the condition itself.

However, hormonal changes, insulin resistance and associated medical conditions can affect the eyes in several ways.

Understanding these risks allows problems to be recognised early and treated appropriately.

---

# What Is PCOS?

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is one of the most common hormonal disorders affecting women of reproductive age.

It is associated with:

- Irregular menstrual cycles
- Increased androgen (male hormone) levels
- Insulin resistance
- Weight gain in some women
- Acne
- Excess facial or body hair
- Difficulty conceiving

Not every woman experiences all of these features.

---

# Can PCOS Affect the Eyes?

Yes.

PCOS may contribute to:

- Dry eye disease
- Meibomian gland dysfunction
- Fluctuating vision
- Increased risk of diabetic eye disease if diabetes develops
- Changes related to obesity or high blood pressure

Most of these effects are indirect rather than caused by PCOS itself.

---

# Dry Eye Disease

Hormones play an important role in maintaining a healthy tear film.

Changes in hormone levels associated with PCOS may contribute to:

- Dryness
- Burning
- Grittiness
- Fluctuating vision
- Contact lens discomfort

Many women with PCOS also spend long hours using digital devices, which can further worsen dry eye symptoms.

---

# Meibomian Gland Dysfunction

The meibomian glands produce the oily layer of the tear film.

Hormonal changes may affect how these glands function.

Poor-quality oil can lead to:

- Faster tear evaporation
- Irritation
- Blurred vision
- Dry eye disease

Treating the eyelids is often an important part of dry eye management.

---

# Diabetes and PCOS

Many women with PCOS have insulin resistance.

Some later develop type 2 diabetes.

Diabetes can damage the small blood vessels in the retina, leading to **diabetic retinopathy**.

Good control of:

- Blood sugar
- Blood pressure
- Cholesterol

helps reduce the risk of diabetic eye disease.

Regular retinal examinations are important for women who develop diabetes.

---

# Weight, Blood Pressure and Eye Health

PCOS is sometimes associated with:

- Obesity
- High blood pressure
- Elevated cholesterol

These conditions increase the risk of several eye diseases, including retinal vascular disease.

Maintaining overall health also benefits long-term eye health.

---

# Can PCOS Cause Blurred Vision?

Sometimes.

Possible reasons include:

- Dry eye disease
- Fluctuating blood sugar
- Temporary changes in the tear film

Persistent blurred vision should always be assessed by an ophthalmologist.

---

# Medications Used for PCOS

Many women with PCOS take medications such as:

- Metformin
- Hormonal contraceptives
- Anti-androgen medications

These medications are generally **not associated with serious eye complications**, but every medication should be considered in the context of your overall health.

Tell your ophthalmologist about all medicines you are taking.

---

# Lifestyle and Eye Health

Healthy lifestyle habits benefit both PCOS and the eyes.

These include:

- Regular physical activity
- A balanced diet
- Maintaining a healthy weight
- Managing blood sugar
- Not smoking
- Adequate sleep
- Staying hydrated

Small improvements in overall health often support healthier eyes.

---

# When Should You Have an Eye Examination?

Arrange an eye examination if you have:

- Persistent blurred vision
- Dry eyes
- Diabetes
- Sudden changes in vision
- Flashes of light
- New floaters
- Eye pain

Women with diabetes should follow the recommended schedule for diabetic retinal screening.

---

# Myths About PCOS and Eye Health

## "PCOS always causes eye disease."

False.

Most women with PCOS never develop serious eye complications.

---

## "Blurred vision is always caused by hormones."

False.

Blurred vision may have many causes, including dry eye, refractive error, cataracts, glaucoma or retinal disease.

---

## "If I have PCOS, I will lose my vision."

False.

With appropriate medical care and regular eye examinations, serious vision loss is uncommon.

---

# My Philosophy

PCOS affects the whole person—not just one organ.

Looking after your eyes is part of looking after your overall health.

The most important approach is prevention.

By managing blood sugar, maintaining a healthy lifestyle and recognising symptoms early, most women can protect both their vision and their long-term wellbeing.

---

# Frequently Asked Questions

## Does PCOS cause dry eyes?

It may contribute to dry eye in some women because hormonal changes can affect the tear film.

---

## Can PCOS affect my glasses prescription?

Temporary fluctuations may occur, particularly if blood sugar levels vary.

Persistent changes should be evaluated.

---

## Do I need regular eye examinations?

Yes.

Especially if you have diabetes, high blood pressure or persistent eye symptoms.

---

## Does metformin damage the eyes?

Metformin is not generally associated with eye damage and may indirectly protect eye health by helping control blood sugar.

---

## Can lifestyle changes improve eye health?

Yes.

Healthy eating, exercise, good blood sugar control and not smoking all support long-term eye health.

---

# Key Take-Home Messages

- PCOS may contribute to dry eye disease and fluctuating vision.
- The greatest long-term eye risks are often related to diabetes and metabolic health rather than PCOS itself.
- Good control of blood sugar, blood pressure and weight helps protect vision.
- Persistent visual symptoms should always be evaluated.
- Regular eye examinations remain an important part of overall health.
