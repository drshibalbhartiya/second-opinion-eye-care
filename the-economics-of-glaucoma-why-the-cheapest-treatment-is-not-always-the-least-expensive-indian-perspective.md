# The Economics of Glaucoma: Why the Cheapest Treatment Is Not Always the Least Expensive (Indian Perspective)

The cost of glaucoma care should never be measured by the price of a bottle of eye drops.

It should be measured by the cost of preserving useful vision over a patient's lifetime.

## Looking Beyond the Pharmacy Bill

In India, treatment decisions are often driven by the monthly cost of medication.

That is understandable.

Glaucoma is a lifelong disease, and affordability determines adherence for many patients.

However, choosing the least expensive medication does not always result in the lowest long-term cost.

The true economic question is:

> **Which treatment preserves vision most effectively while remaining sustainable for the patient?**

## Generic Versus Branded Medication

Generic medications have an important role in glaucoma care and improve access for millions of patients.

However, not all generic formulations perform identically.

In India, bottle design, drop size, preservatives and manufacturing consistency may vary between manufacturers.

These differences may influence:

- patient comfort
- adherence
- number of usable drops per bottle
- consistency of intraocular pressure control

A bottle that lasts only fifteen or twenty days instead of approximately one month may not be less expensive after all.

Similarly, if inadequate pressure control requires adding a second or third medication, the apparent saving from the initial prescription may disappear.

The objective should never be to prescribe the cheapest drop.

It should be to prescribe the **most cost-effective** treatment for that individual patient.

## Measuring Cost Properly

The economics of glaucoma extend far beyond medication costs.

As glaucoma progresses, patients may lose independence, require family support, stop driving, fall more frequently and eventually require surgical intervention.

These consequences are reflected in concepts such as:

- Quality-Adjusted Life Years (QALYs)
- Disability-Adjusted Life Years (DALYs)

These measures remind us that preserving vision preserves quality of life, productivity and independence.

When these broader outcomes are considered, early and effective treatment is often more economical than delayed intervention.

## The Value of Selective Laser Trabeculoplasty

Selective Laser Trabeculoplasty (SLT) is one of the most cost-effective treatments available for appropriately selected patients with open-angle glaucoma.

It reduces dependence on long-term topical medication and avoids many of the adherence challenges associated with daily eye drops.

For many patients, SLT represents an investment that may reduce treatment costs over several years.

## Where MIGS Fits

Minimally Invasive Glaucoma Surgery (MIGS) has expanded the range of treatment options available for glaucoma.

Its greatest value lies in carefully selected patients, particularly when combined with cataract surgery or when reducing medication burden is an important goal.

However, MIGS should not automatically be viewed as the most economical option.

The higher procedural cost must be balanced against the expected reduction in medications, follow-up requirements and long-term disease control.

Like every glaucoma intervention, value depends on selecting the right patient.

## The Economics of Traditional Glaucoma Surgery

Trabeculectomy and glaucoma drainage devices remain among the most effective procedures for lowering intraocular pressure.

Their economic impact, however, extends beyond the operating theatre.

Follow-up visits, postoperative interventions, bleb management, cataract progression, occasional reoperations and long-term surveillance all contribute to the overall cost of care.

These procedures often provide tremendous value when appropriately indicated.

Their true cost is measured over years rather than on the day of surgery.

## My Perspective

Every glaucoma decision has both a clinical cost and an economic cost.

The goal is not to minimise today's expenditure.

The goal is to maximise lifelong visual function while using healthcare resources wisely.

In glaucoma, the most economical treatment is often the one that prevents progression before it occurs.

Sometimes that treatment is a generic medication.

Sometimes it is an innovative drug.

Sometimes it is SLT.

Sometimes it is surgery.

The best decision is rarely the least expensive today.

It is the one that provides the greatest value over a lifetime.
