# Understanding Your Eye Tests

> **Eye tests are not about collecting numbers. They help us answer important questions about your vision, eye health, and the best way to protect your sight over time.**

Many patients leave an eye appointment carrying several reports but are unsure what they mean.

An OCT scan.

A visual field printout.

An eye pressure measurement.

A photograph of the optic nerve.

It can all seem overwhelming.

This guide explains the purpose of the most common eye tests, why your ophthalmologist recommends them, and how they fit together to guide diagnosis and treatment.

---

# Why Do Eye Doctors Perform So Many Tests?

Every test provides a different piece of information.

No single test can diagnose every eye condition.

Think of your eye examination as putting together a jigsaw puzzle.

One test measures eye pressure.

Another measures vision.

Another examines the optic nerve.

Another assesses the retina.

Together, these findings help your ophthalmologist understand what is happening inside your eyes.

---

# A Normal Test Does Not Always Mean a Normal Eye

This surprises many people.

One normal test does **not** rule out eye disease.

For example:

- You can have glaucoma with normal eye pressure.
- You can have a normal OCT but an abnormal visual field.
- You can have excellent vision but early glaucoma.
- You can have dry eye despite a normal eye examination.

Your ophthalmologist interprets all your test results together rather than relying on any single report.

---

# Common Eye Tests

## Vision Test (Visual Acuity)

This measures how clearly you see letters at a standard distance.

It helps determine:

- Whether glasses are needed
- How well each eye sees
- Whether vision has changed over time

Good vision does not always mean healthy eyes.

---

## Refraction

Refraction determines the spectacle prescription needed to provide the clearest possible vision.

It does not diagnose eye disease.

---

## Eye Pressure (Tonometry)

Eye pressure is measured because elevated pressure increases the risk of glaucoma.

However:

- Some people develop glaucoma despite normal eye pressure.
- Others have high eye pressure but never develop glaucoma.

Eye pressure is only one part of the picture.

**Read more:** [Understanding Eye Pressure](./understanding-eye-pressure.md)

---

## Slit Lamp Examination

The slit lamp is a specialised microscope that allows your ophthalmologist to examine:

- Eyelids
- Tear film
- Cornea
- Iris
- Lens
- Front part of the eye

It helps diagnose many common eye conditions.

---

## Dilated Eye Examination

Eye drops enlarge the pupil so the retina and optic nerve can be examined thoroughly.

This helps detect:

- Glaucoma
- Diabetic eye disease
- Retinal tears
- Macular degeneration
- Retinal vascular disease

---

## Optical Coherence Tomography (OCT)

OCT creates detailed cross-sectional images of the retina and optic nerve.

It helps detect structural changes that may not yet affect vision.

OCT is commonly used for:

- Glaucoma
- Macular degeneration
- Diabetic eye disease
- Retinal swelling

An OCT scan should always be interpreted alongside your examination and other tests.

**Read more:** [How to Read Your OCT Report](./how-to-read-your-oct-report.md)

---

## Visual Field Test

Visual field testing measures your side vision.

It helps detect areas of vision loss that may not be noticeable in everyday life.

This test is especially important for glaucoma.

Visual field results should be interpreted over time rather than relying on a single test.

**Read more:** [How to Read Your Visual Field Report](./how-to-read-your-visual-field-report.md)

---

## Fundus Photography

Colour photographs of the retina provide a permanent record that can be compared over time.

This helps monitor changes in:

- The optic nerve
- Retina
- Blood vessels

---

## Pachymetry

Pachymetry measures the thickness of the cornea.

This information helps your ophthalmologist interpret eye pressure measurements more accurately.

It is particularly useful in glaucoma assessment.

---

## Gonioscopy

Gonioscopy examines the drainage angle inside the eye.

It helps determine:

- Whether the angle is open or closed
- The type of glaucoma
- Whether laser treatment may be appropriate

Although it sounds complicated, the test is usually quick and well tolerated.

---

## Corneal Topography

This test maps the shape of the cornea.

It is commonly used before:

- LASIK
- Contact lens fitting
- Corneal disease assessment

---

## Colour Vision Testing

Colour vision testing may be used when evaluating:

- Optic nerve disease
- Inherited colour vision deficiency
- Certain retinal disorders

---

## Ultrasound

Ultrasound allows doctors to examine structures inside the eye when they cannot be seen directly, such as when a dense cataract blocks the view.

---

# Why Are Repeat Tests Necessary?

Eye diseases change over time.

A single scan provides only one snapshot.

Repeat testing allows your ophthalmologist to determine:

- Whether the disease is stable
- Whether treatment is working
- Whether progression has occurred

Often, the trend is more important than the individual result.

---

# Why Different Doctors May Order Different Tests

Not every patient requires every test.

The investigations recommended depend on:

- Your symptoms
- Your diagnosis
- Your age
- Your medical history
- Previous results

Testing should answer a clinical question—not simply generate more reports.

# My Philosophy

I believe patients should understand their investigations.

Eye tests should never feel mysterious.

Every test should answer a specific question that helps guide diagnosis or treatment.

My goal is not simply to perform investigations.

---

# Frequently Asked Questions

## Which eye test is the most important?

There isn't one.

Different tests answer different questions.

---

## Why do I need repeated OCT scans?

To determine whether changes are occurring over time.

---

## Can one abnormal test diagnose glaucoma?

Usually not.

Diagnosis depends on the complete clinical picture.

---

## Why do my reports look different at different clinics?

Different machines, techniques and software may produce different formats.

Your ophthalmologist interprets the results in context.

---

## Should I keep copies of my reports?

Yes.

Previous reports are often extremely valuable for comparison.

---

# Key Take-Home Messages

- Every eye test answers a different clinical question.
- No single investigation diagnoses every eye condition.
- Trends over time are often more important than individual results.
- Understanding your reports helps you participate in your own care.
- Always discuss test results with your ophthalmologist rather than interpreting them in isolation.
