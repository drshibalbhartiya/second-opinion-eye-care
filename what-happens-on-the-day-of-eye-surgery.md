# What Happens on the Day of Eye Surgery?

> **Most eye operations are much less intimidating than patients imagine. Knowing what to expect can make the experience calmer and less stressful.**

Many people feel anxious before eye surgery.

Common questions include:

- Will I be awake?
- Will I feel pain?
- How long will it take?
- Will I be able to see during surgery?
- When can I go home?

Although every procedure is different, most eye operations follow a similar journey.

---

# Before You Leave Home

On the day of surgery:

- Follow any fasting instructions you have been given.
- Take your regular medications unless your doctor has advised otherwise.
- Wear comfortable clothing.
- Avoid eye makeup, perfumes and aftershave if instructed.
- Bring your identification, medical records and medications.
- Arrange for someone to accompany you home if advised.

Follow the instructions provided by your surgical team, as they may differ depending on the procedure.

---

# Arriving at the Hospital

After you arrive:

- Your details will be confirmed.
- Your medical history will be reviewed.
- Your eye will be checked again.
- The correct eye will be confirmed.
- Your blood pressure and other routine observations may be recorded.

This is also an opportunity to ask any last-minute questions.

---

# Preparing for Surgery

Before the operation:

- Eye drops are often used to dilate the pupil or prepare the eye.
- The area around the eye is cleaned carefully.
- You may receive medication to help you relax if appropriate.

Several safety checks are performed before surgery begins.

---

# Will I Be Awake?

For many eye operations, including cataract surgery, patients remain awake.

The eye is usually numbed with anaesthetic eye drops, an injection around the eye, or both.

You should not feel pain, although you may notice:

- Gentle pressure
- Bright lights
- Colours
- Movement

You will not be expected to watch the operation.

---

# Inside the Operating Theatre

The operating theatre is carefully organised to maintain safety and sterility.

You will usually:

- Lie comfortably on your back.
- Have the area around the eye cleaned.
- Be covered with sterile drapes that leave the eye exposed.
- Be asked to look towards a bright light.

Your surgical team will explain each step as appropriate.

---

# During Surgery

The length of surgery depends on the procedure.

Many common eye operations are completed within a relatively short time.

Your surgeon and nurses will continue talking to you throughout the operation if needed.

If you feel uncomfortable or anxious, let the team know.

---

# Will I Be Able to See?

Many patients notice:

- Bright lights
- Colours
- Shadows
- Movement

These experiences are normal.

Most people do **not** see the surgical instruments or the details of the operation.

---

# After Surgery

Following the procedure:

- Your eye may be covered with a protective shield or dressing.
- You will spend some time in the recovery area.
- Nurses will monitor you before discharge.
- You will receive instructions about eye drops and activity.

Most patients go home the same day.

---

# Going Home

Before leaving, you will usually receive advice about:

- Eye drops
- Protective eye shields
- Bathing and washing
- Physical activity
- Sleeping positions (if relevant)
- Follow-up appointments
- When to seek urgent medical attention

Take time to read these instructions carefully.

---

# Is It Normal to Feel Strange Afterwards?

Yes.

You may notice:

- Mild watering
- A scratchy sensation
- Slight redness
- Temporary blurred vision
- Sensitivity to light

These symptoms often improve during the early stages of recovery.

Your surgeon will explain what to expect for your specific procedure.

---

# When Should You Contact Your Doctor?

Seek urgent medical advice if you develop:

- Severe eye pain
- Sudden loss of vision
- Increasing redness
- Thick discharge
- Persistent nausea or vomiting
- New flashes or a curtain across your vision (if advised)

Do not wait until your next scheduled appointment if you are concerned.

---

# Tips for a Smoother Experience

Many patients find it helpful to:

- Get a good night's sleep before surgery.
- Wear comfortable clothing.
- Arrive on time.
- Bring a family member or friend if advised.
- Ask questions whenever something is unclear.

Knowing what to expect often reduces anxiety.

---

# My Philosophy

The day of surgery is about much more than the operation itself.

It is about helping patients feel informed, reassured and safe.

Good surgery begins long before entering the operating theatre and continues throughout recovery.

Every member of the team has a role in making your experience as comfortable as possible.

---

# Frequently Asked Questions

## Will the operation hurt?

Modern eye surgery is usually performed with effective anaesthesia, and most patients experience little or no pain during the procedure.

---

## Can I eat before surgery?

Follow the specific instructions provided by your surgical team.

These vary depending on the procedure and the type of anaesthesia.

---

## How long will I stay in hospital?

Most eye operations are performed as day-care procedures, allowing patients to return home the same day.

---

## Will I need someone to take me home?

For many procedures, yes.

Your hospital will advise you before surgery.

---

## Can I wear makeup?

Avoid eye makeup on the day of surgery unless your surgical team advises otherwise.

---

# Key Take-Home Messages

- Most eye surgery is performed as a day-care procedure.
- Modern anaesthesia makes surgery comfortable for most patients.
- You may notice lights and movement during surgery, but not pain.
- Careful preparation and follow-up are important parts of successful treatment.
- If you experience severe pain or sudden vision loss after surgery, seek urgent medical attention.
