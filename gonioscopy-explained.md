# Gonioscopy Explained: Why Your Eye Doctor Examines the Drainage Angle

> **Gonioscopy is one of the most important examinations in glaucoma care. It helps your ophthalmologist understand how fluid leaves your eye and whether you are at risk of certain types of glaucoma.**

Many patients have never heard of gonioscopy until their doctor places a small lens on the eye during an examination.

It is natural to wonder:

- Why do I need this test?
- Is it painful?
- What does it show?
- Why can't an OCT or eye pressure measurement replace it?

The answer is simple.

Gonioscopy allows your ophthalmologist to examine a part of the eye that cannot be seen any other way during a routine examination.

---

# What Is Gonioscopy?

Gonioscopy is a clinical examination of the **drainage angle** of the eye.

The drainage angle is where the clear fluid inside the eye (aqueous humour) leaves the eye.

If this drainage system does not function normally, eye pressure may increase.

---

# Why Is the Drainage Angle Important?

Your eye continuously produces fluid.

That fluid must drain away at approximately the same rate that it is produced.

If drainage becomes reduced or blocked:

- Eye pressure may increase.
- The optic nerve may become damaged.
- Glaucoma may develop.

Understanding the drainage angle helps determine **why** eye pressure behaves the way it does.

---

# Why Is Gonioscopy Performed?

Your ophthalmologist may recommend gonioscopy to:

- Diagnose glaucoma
- Distinguish between different types of glaucoma
- Assess narrow angles
- Detect angle closure
- Evaluate ocular hypertension
- Examine abnormal blood vessels
- Look for pigment or inflammation
- Assess previous eye injury

It is considered an essential part of a comprehensive glaucoma examination.

---

# How Is Gonioscopy Performed?

The examination is simple.

Your ophthalmologist will:

- Instil anaesthetic eye drops.
- Ask you to sit at the slit lamp microscope.
- Gently place a special contact lens on the surface of the eye.
- Examine the drainage angle using the microscope.

The examination usually takes only a few minutes.

---

# Is Gonioscopy Painful?

No.

Because anaesthetic drops are used, most people feel only mild pressure from the examination lens.

Many patients are surprised by how comfortable the test is.

---

# What Does Your Doctor Look For?

During gonioscopy, your ophthalmologist evaluates:

- Whether the drainage angle is open or narrow
- The amount of pigmentation
- Signs of previous inflammation
- Abnormal blood vessels
- Scar tissue
- Damage after trauma
- Evidence of angle closure

Each of these findings helps guide diagnosis and treatment.

---

# Open Angle vs Closed Angle

One of the most important questions gonioscopy answers is:

**Is the drainage angle open or closed?**

### Open-Angle Glaucoma

The drainage angle appears open, but fluid does not drain efficiently.

This is the most common form of glaucoma.

---

### Angle-Closure Glaucoma

The drainage angle becomes narrow or blocked.

Fluid cannot leave the eye normally.

Eye pressure may rise suddenly or gradually.

Some cases require urgent treatment.

---

# Can OCT Replace Gonioscopy?

No.

OCT and gonioscopy provide different information.

OCT examines the optic nerve and retina.

Gonioscopy examines the drainage angle.

Both tests are valuable, but neither replaces the other.

---

# Why Is Gonioscopy Important Even if My Eye Pressure Is Normal?

Eye pressure can change throughout the day.

A normal pressure reading does not rule out angle closure or other abnormalities.

Gonioscopy provides anatomical information that pressure measurements alone cannot.

---

# Can Gonioscopy Predict Future Problems?

Sometimes.

If the drainage angle is very narrow, your ophthalmologist may identify a risk of future angle closure before symptoms develop.

This allows treatment to be planned before an emergency occurs.

---

# Do I Need Gonioscopy at Every Visit?

Not necessarily.

The frequency depends on:

- Your diagnosis
- Your risk of glaucoma
- Previous findings
- Whether the angle appears stable

Some patients require periodic reassessment.

Others need it only occasionally.

---

# Are There Any Risks?

Gonioscopy is a very safe examination.

Temporary blurred vision from the anaesthetic drops or examination gel may occur for a few minutes.

Serious complications are extremely rare.

---

# Common Myths

## "Gonioscopy is a surgery."

False.

It is simply an examination performed in the clinic.

---

## "If my OCT is normal, I don't need gonioscopy."

False.

The two tests examine completely different parts of the eye.

---

## "A normal eye pressure means my drainage angle is normal."

False.

The drainage angle may still be narrow or abnormal despite normal eye pressure.

---

## "The test damages the eye."

False.

When performed correctly, gonioscopy is a safe and routine examination.

---

# Questions to Ask Your Ophthalmologist

You may wish to ask:

- Is my drainage angle open or narrow?
- Am I at risk of angle-closure glaucoma?
- Do I need laser treatment?
- Will I need repeat gonioscopy?
- Does my angle affect my glaucoma treatment?

---

# My Philosophy

Gonioscopy is one of the few examinations that cannot be replaced by technology.

It allows us to directly examine the part of the eye responsible for fluid drainage.

Every glaucoma patient deserves a careful assessment of the drainage angle.

The findings often explain why eye pressure behaves the way it does and help determine the safest long-term treatment plan.

Technology is valuable.

Clinical examination remains essential.

---

# Frequently Asked Questions

## Does gonioscopy hurt?

No.

Anaesthetic eye drops make the examination comfortable.

---

## How long does it take?

Usually only a few minutes.

---

## Can I drive afterwards?

Most people can.

Your vision may be slightly blurred for a short time because of the examination gel or drops.

---

## Is gonioscopy necessary if I already had an OCT?

Yes.

The tests examine different parts of the eye.

---

## Can gonioscopy diagnose glaucoma?

It helps diagnose the type of glaucoma, but the diagnosis always depends on the complete clinical picture.

---

# Key Take-Home Messages

- Gonioscopy examines the drainage angle of the eye.
- It is essential for diagnosing different types of glaucoma.
- It cannot be replaced by OCT or eye pressure measurements.
- The examination is quick, safe and usually painless.
- Understanding the drainage angle helps guide the best treatment plan.
