# How to Put Eye Drops in Correctly

> **Using your eye drops correctly is part of your treatment. Even the best medication cannot work properly if it never reaches your eye.**

Many patients are surprised to learn that eye drop technique is a clinical outcome.

A medication only works if it reaches the eye in the right amount, at the right time, and consistently.

Poor technique is one of the most common—and most easily corrected—reasons why treatment may not work as expected.

---

# Why Does Technique Matter?

Eye drops are prescribed carefully.

However, many people:

- Miss the eye completely
- Instil multiple drops at once
- Blink the medication out immediately
- Wash the drop away with tears
- Contaminate the bottle tip
- Forget doses

Small improvements in technique can make treatment more effective.

---

# Before You Start

Always:

- Wash your hands thoroughly.
- Check the label to make sure you have the correct medication.
- Check the expiry date.
- Shake the bottle if instructed.
- Remove contact lenses if advised.

---

# Step 1: Find a Comfortable Position

You may:

- Sit in a chair.
- Stand in front of a mirror.
- Lie down on a bed or sofa.

Choose whichever position allows you to feel steady and relaxed.

---

# Step 2: Tilt Your Head Back

Look upwards.

Try to keep your eyes relaxed.

Avoid squeezing your eyelids tightly.

---

# Step 3: Create a Small Pocket

Using one finger, gently pull the lower eyelid down.

This creates a small pocket where the drop can land.

---

# Step 4: Hold the Bottle Above the Eye

Hold the bottle close to the eye, but **do not allow the tip to touch:**

- Your eye
- Your eyelashes
- Your eyelids
- Your fingers

Keeping the bottle clean helps prevent contamination.

---

# Step 5: Instil One Drop

Gently squeeze the bottle until **one drop** falls into the lower eyelid pocket.

One drop is enough.

The eye can hold only a very small volume of liquid.

Extra drops usually overflow onto the cheek rather than improving treatment.

---

# Step 6: Close Your Eye Gently

Close your eye gently for about **one to two minutes**.

Do not squeeze your eyelids tightly.

Gentle closure allows the medication to remain on the surface of the eye for longer.

---

# Step 7: Press the Inner Corner of the Eye

Using a clean finger, gently press the inner corner of the eyelids, next to the nose, for about **one to two minutes**.

This is called **punctal occlusion**.

It helps:

- Keep more medication in the eye
- Reduce drainage into the nose
- Reduce absorption into the bloodstream
- Reduce the risk of systemic side effects

This step is especially important for many glaucoma medications.

---

# Using More Than One Eye Drop

If you use several eye drops:

Wait at least **five minutes** between different medications.

This prevents the second drop from washing away the first.

If you also use an eye ointment or gel:

Apply the drops first.

Use the ointment last.

---

# What If I Miss the Eye?

Don't worry.

Simply try again.

Avoid repeatedly squeezing the bottle, as this often wastes medication.

With practice, most people become much more confident.

---

# Common Mistakes

Common problems include:

- Using more than one drop
- Touching the bottle tip to the eye
- Not washing hands
- Forgetting doses
- Stopping treatment without medical advice
- Not waiting between different eye drops
- Squeezing the eyes tightly afterwards
- Forgetting punctal occlusion

Fortunately, all of these can be corrected.

---

# Tips for Glaucoma Patients

If you have glaucoma:

- Use your drops at the same time every day.
- Follow the schedule recommended by your ophthalmologist.
- Never stop treatment without medical advice.
- Tell your doctor if you struggle to use your drops.

There are often practical solutions that can help.

---

# What If I Cannot Put My Drops In?

Many people find eye drops difficult because of:

- Arthritis
- Tremor
- Poor vision
- Reduced hand strength
- Neck problems

If this applies to you:

- Ask a family member for help.
- Discuss eye drop aids with your ophthalmologist.
- Tell your doctor if you regularly miss doses.

Difficulty using eye drops is a medical issue—not something to feel embarrassed about.

---

# Looking After Your Eye Drops

Store your medication exactly as instructed.

Some drops require refrigeration before opening.

Others can be stored at room temperature.

Always:

- Replace the cap immediately after use.
- Keep bottles clean.
- Do not share eye drops with anyone else.
- Discard the bottle according to the manufacturer's instructions.

---

# My Philosophy

Teaching patients how to use eye drops correctly is not simply education.

It is part of treatment.

Time spent demonstrating eye drop technique is a clinical intervention that can improve outcomes just as much as changing a prescription.

The right medication matters.

Using it correctly matters just as much.

---

# Frequently Asked Questions

## Is one drop enough?

Yes.

One drop is sufficient for almost all eye medications.

---

## Should I blink after putting the drop in?

Close your eye gently.

Avoid repeated blinking or squeezing your eyelids tightly.

---

## How long should I wait between different eye drops?

Usually at least five minutes.

---

## Why do the drops taste bitter?

Some medication drains into the nose and throat.

Punctal occlusion can reduce this.

---

## What should I do if I forget a dose?

Follow the advice given by your ophthalmologist or the medication instructions.

Do not double the next dose unless specifically advised.

---

# Key Take-Home Messages

- Correct eye drop technique improves treatment effectiveness.
- One drop is enough.
- Avoid touching the bottle tip to your eye.
- Close your eyes gently after instilling the drop.
- Pressing the inner corner of the eye helps reduce side effects.
- Learning proper technique is an important part of successful treatment.
