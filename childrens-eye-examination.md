# Children's Eye Examination: What to Expect

> **Children do not always know when they have a vision problem. A routine eye examination can detect conditions long before they begin affecting learning or development.**

Many parents assume that if a child can read, watch television or use a tablet, their vision must be normal.

Unfortunately, this is not always true.

Children often adapt remarkably well to poor vision because they have never experienced anything different.

Regular eye examinations help detect problems early, when treatment is often most effective.

---

# Why Does My Child Need an Eye Examination?

Good vision is essential for:

- Learning
- Reading
- Writing
- Playing sports
- Hand-eye coordination
- Social development
- Confidence

Many childhood eye conditions can be treated successfully if detected early.

---

# When Should Children Have Their Eyes Checked?

An eye examination is recommended:

- If there are concerns about vision
- If a teacher notices difficulties
- If there is a family history of eye disease
- If your child was born prematurely
- After an eye injury
- If your child develops a squint (strabismus)

Your ophthalmologist will advise how often follow-up is needed.

---

# What Happens During a Children's Eye Examination?

Every examination is tailored to the child's age and development.

It may include:

- Assessment of visual acuity
- Eye alignment testing
- Eye movement assessment
- Colour vision testing (when appropriate)
- Refraction to determine the need for glasses
- Examination of the front of the eye
- Examination of the retina and optic nerve
- Assessment for amblyopia (lazy eye)

The examination is usually painless.

---

# Does My Child Need to Know Letters?

No.

There are many ways to assess vision in young children.

Depending on age, we may use:

- Pictures
- Matching games
- Shapes
- Symbols
- Age-appropriate vision charts

Even babies can have their vision assessed.

---

# Will My Child Need Eye Drops?

Sometimes.

Dilating eye drops may be recommended to:

- Examine the retina
- Accurately measure the need for glasses
- Assess certain eye conditions

Your ophthalmologist will explain if these are needed.

---

# Common Problems Found During Eye Examinations

An examination may identify:

- Refractive errors (short-sightedness, long-sightedness and astigmatism)
- Amblyopia (lazy eye)
- Strabismus (squint)
- Myopia progression
- Allergies
- Congenital eye conditions
- Less commonly, glaucoma or retinal disease

Most children do not have serious eye disease.

---

# Signs Your Child May Have a Vision Problem

Watch for:

- Sitting very close to the television
- Holding books very close
- Frequent headaches
- Squinting
- One eye turning in or out
- Excessive blinking
- Rubbing the eyes frequently
- Poor school performance
- Difficulty catching a ball
- Complaints of blurred vision

These symptoms deserve an eye examination.

---

# What If My Child Needs Glasses?

Many children adapt to glasses quickly.

Glasses do not weaken the eyes.

They simply allow your child to see clearly and comfortably.

---

# Can Eye Examinations Prevent Vision Loss?

Early diagnosis often allows treatment before permanent vision problems develop.

This is particularly important for conditions such as amblyopia, where treatment is most effective during childhood.

---

# My Philosophy

A child's eye examination is about much more than prescribing glasses.

It is an opportunity to ensure that vision supports learning, development and confidence.

Children rarely complain that they cannot see well.

It is our responsibility to recognise problems before they begin affecting their future.

---

# Frequently Asked Questions

## Are children's eye examinations painful?

No.

Most tests are simple, interactive and painless.

---

## At what age can vision be tested?

Vision can be assessed from infancy using age-appropriate techniques.

---

## Can children have glaucoma?

Yes, although it is uncommon.

Congenital and childhood glaucoma require specialist care.

---

## Will wearing glasses make my child's eyes weaker?

No.

Glasses improve vision but do not weaken the eyes.

---

## How long does the examination take?

The length varies depending on your child's age and the tests required.

---

# Key Take-Home Messages

- Children do not always recognise vision problems.
- Regular eye examinations help detect problems early.
- Many childhood eye conditions are highly treatable.
- Good vision supports learning and development.
- Never ignore persistent visual symptoms or a squint.
