
# Signature Clinical Frameworks by Dr Shibal Bhartiya

Original clinical methodologies developed by Dr Shibal Bhartiya for structured assessment, personalised treatment and long-term vision preservation.

These frameworks integrate comprehensive clinical assessment, diagnostic investigations, individual risk profiling and evidence-based treatment strategies. They are designed to bring greater structure and consistency to complex clinical decisions while recognising that every patient has a different disease profile, risk trajectory and treatment requirement.

Each framework provides a systematic approach to moving beyond symptom-based management towards understanding the underlying disease, identifying modifiable risk factors and developing a meaningful, individualised plan of care.

---

## 1. L.I.F.T.S.™ – Glaucoma Second Opinion Framework

The L.I.F.T.S.™ framework is a structured approach to reviewing glaucoma diagnoses, evaluating disease progression and developing individualised management strategies.

Glaucoma is a chronic, progressive condition in which treatment decisions must account not only for current intraocular pressure and optic nerve status, but also for the patient's lifetime risk of visual disability. A single examination or isolated test result may not adequately reflect the course of the disease.

L.I.F.T.S.™ brings together longitudinal clinical information, independent diagnostic review, functional assessment and lifetime risk profiling to support more informed glaucoma management.

**The five steps are:**

- **Longitudinal Record Integration** – Reviewing previous clinical records, investigations, treatment history and changes over time.
- **Independent Diagnostic Re-Evaluation** – Reassessing the diagnosis and considering alternative explanations for the observed findings.
- **Functional Rate-of-Progression Analysis** – Evaluating visual field changes and structural progression to understand the rate of disease progression.
- **Target IOP and Lifetime Risk Profiling** – Establishing an individualised target intraocular pressure based on disease severity, progression and lifetime risk.
- **Proportional and Sustainable Management Planning** – Developing a treatment strategy that balances disease control, treatment burden, safety and long-term adherence.

## 2. C.L.E.A.R.™ – Dry Eye Second Opinion Framework

The C.L.E.A.R.™ framework is a structured approach to understanding the underlying causes of dry eye disease rather than treating symptoms in isolation.

Dry eye disease is multifactorial. Tear film instability, meibomian gland dysfunction, ocular surface inflammation, environmental exposures and systemic conditions may contribute in different combinations. Persistent symptoms often require a more detailed evaluation than simply escalating lubricating eye drops.

C.L.E.A.R.™ encourages a systematic assessment of tear film function, glandular health, environmental and systemic contributors, inflammation and the factors that prevent sustained improvement.

**The five steps are:**

- **Comprehensive Aetiology** – Identifying the different mechanisms and contributing factors responsible for ocular surface disease.
- **Lipid and Gland Mapping** – Evaluating the lipid layer, meibomian gland structure and function, and related tear film abnormalities.
- **Environmental and Systemic Triage** – Assessing environmental exposures, medications, systemic conditions and lifestyle factors that may aggravate dry eye.
- **Advanced Inflammation Profiling** – Evaluating the role of ocular surface inflammation and identifying patients who may need targeted anti-inflammatory treatment.
- **Root-Cause Rehabilitation** – Developing a personalised plan to address underlying mechanisms, restore ocular surface health and maintain long-term improvement.

## 3. S.L.O.W.™ – Paediatric Myopia Control Framework

The S.L.O.W.™ framework is a structured approach to assessing childhood myopia, identifying progression risk and developing individualised strategies to slow axial elongation.

Childhood myopia is not simply a refractive error requiring periodic spectacle changes. The age of onset, rate of progression, axial length, family history, visual environment and individual risk profile all influence the likelihood of developing high myopia and its associated long-term complications.

S.L.O.W.™ provides a systematic approach to baseline assessment, risk stratification and the selection of appropriate optical, pharmacological and environmental interventions. It also emphasises the importance of monitoring progression over time and adapting treatment as the child grows.

**The four steps are:**

- **Structural Baseline Mapping** – Establishing the refractive and ocular structural baseline, including axial length assessment where available.
- **Lifetime Risk and Ethnicity Profiling** – Considering age of onset, family history, progression patterns and relevant demographic and lifestyle risk factors.
- **Optical Intervention Design** – Selecting appropriate myopia-control optical strategies based on the child's clinical profile and treatment needs.
- **Wet and World Shield** – Integrating pharmacological approaches where indicated with environmental measures, including outdoor time and healthier visual habits.

## A Common Clinical Philosophy

Although these frameworks address different clinical conditions, they share a common philosophy: good clinical care requires a structured understanding of the individual patient, not just a response to an isolated test result or symptom.

The principles common to all three frameworks include:

- **Understand the underlying disease:** Identify the mechanisms driving the condition rather than treating symptoms alone.
- **Establish an objective clinical baseline:** Use appropriate examination findings and diagnostic investigations to document the starting point.
- **Identify individual risk factors:** Consider disease severity, progression, patient characteristics and long-term implications.
- **Integrate diagnostic information:** Interpret investigations in the context of the clinical picture rather than in isolation.
- **Develop personalised treatment strategies:** Match interventions to the patient's needs, risks, preferences and likely benefit.
- **Monitor outcomes longitudinally:** Reassess response to treatment and modify the plan when clinical findings or risk profiles change.
- **Balance efficacy with sustainability:** Aim for meaningful long-term outcomes while considering safety, adherence and treatment burden.

The objective is to make complex clinical decisions more structured, transparent and patient-centred. These frameworks are intended to support clinical reasoning and individualised care; they do not replace clinical judgement or the need to adapt management to each patient's circumstances.
