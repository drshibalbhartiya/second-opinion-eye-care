# Why Good Vision Does Not Always Mean Safe Vision

You can read the chart.  
You can see clearly.  
Your eye test says everything is normal.

So why does something still feel off?

---

## The assumption most people make

If you can see 6/6 (or 20/20), your eyes must be healthy.

This is one of the most common—and most misleading—assumptions in eye care.

Because **vision clarity is not the same as eye health.**

---

## What the chart actually tests

A standard vision chart measures:
- How clearly you can see high-contrast letters  
- At a fixed distance  
- In ideal lighting  

It does **not** test:
- Peripheral vision  
- Early glaucoma damage  
- Night vision difficulties  
- Contrast sensitivity  
- Real-world visual strain  

---

## Where problems get missed

Many eye conditions begin silently.

Especially:
- **Glaucoma** – often called the “silent thief of sight”  
- Early neurological visual issues  
- Functional vision problems  

In these cases:
- Your central vision may remain normal  
- Your reports may look “fine”  
- But damage may already be starting  

---

## Real-world signs patients ignore

You may still need a closer look if you notice:

- Difficulty driving at night  
- Glare or halos around lights  
- Eye strain despite normal reports  
- Feeling that vision is “not comfortable”  
- Subtle changes that don’t show on routine tests  

---

## Why this gap exists

Most routine eye exams are designed to:
- Detect obvious refractive errors  
- Provide glasses  
- Screen basic eye health  

They are not always designed to detect:
👉 **early, subtle, or functional problems**

---

## When to consider a second opinion

You should not ignore your symptoms just because your report is “normal.”

A second opinion helps when:
- Symptoms persist despite reassurance  
- You feel something is being missed  
- There is a family history of glaucoma  
- Your daily visual experience does not match your test results  

---

## The core idea

**Seeing well is not the same as being safe.**

And the earlier we recognise this gap,  
the more we can prevent irreversible loss.
