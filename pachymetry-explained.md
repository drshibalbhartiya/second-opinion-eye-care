# Pachymetry Explained: Why Corneal Thickness Matters

> **Corneal thickness does not cause glaucoma—but it helps us understand your eye pressure more accurately and estimate your risk of developing glaucoma.**

Many patients are surprised when their ophthalmologist measures the thickness of the cornea.

"What does the thickness of my cornea have to do with glaucoma?"

The answer is:

Quite a lot.

Corneal thickness affects how eye pressure is measured and provides important information when assessing glaucoma risk.

---

# What Is Pachymetry?

Pachymetry is the measurement of **corneal thickness**.

The cornea is the clear, dome-shaped window at the front of your eye.

Although it is only about half a millimetre thick, small differences in thickness can influence the interpretation of eye pressure measurements.

---

# Why Is Pachymetry Performed?

Your ophthalmologist may recommend pachymetry to:

- Better interpret eye pressure
- Assess glaucoma risk
- Evaluate ocular hypertension
- Monitor certain corneal diseases
- Plan refractive surgery such as LASIK

It is a routine part of many glaucoma assessments.

---

# How Is Pachymetry Done?

Pachymetry is quick and painless.

It may be performed using:

- Ultrasound pachymetry
- Optical (non-contact) pachymetry

The test usually takes only a few minutes.

---

# What Is a Normal Corneal Thickness?

Corneal thickness varies naturally between individuals.

Most healthy corneas measure approximately **520–560 microns (µm)** in the centre.

Some people naturally have thinner corneas.

Others naturally have thicker corneas.

Neither is automatically abnormal.

---

# Why Does Corneal Thickness Matter?

Most eye pressure measurements are influenced by the thickness of the cornea.

A:

- Thin cornea may lead to an eye pressure reading that is lower than the true pressure.
- Thick cornea may lead to an eye pressure reading that is higher than the true pressure.

This is one reason why your ophthalmologist never interprets eye pressure in isolation.

---

# Does a Thin Cornea Mean I Have Glaucoma?

No.

A thin cornea does **not** mean you have glaucoma.

However, research has shown that people with thinner corneas may have a higher risk of developing glaucoma or glaucoma progression.

Corneal thickness is **one** risk factor among many.

---

# Does a Thick Cornea Protect Against Glaucoma?

Not necessarily.

A thick cornea does not prevent glaucoma.

It simply means that eye pressure measurements may need to be interpreted differently.

People with thick corneas can still develop glaucoma.

---

# Why Can't My Eye Pressure Be "Corrected"?

You may read online about formulas that "correct" eye pressure based on corneal thickness.

In reality, glaucoma specialists rarely rely on simple correction formulas.

Corneal thickness influences eye pressure measurements, but it is **not the only factor**.

Corneal properties such as elasticity and biomechanics also play a role.

Your ophthalmologist considers the whole clinical picture rather than applying a mathematical correction.

---

# Corneal Thickness Is Only One Piece of the Puzzle

When assessing glaucoma, your ophthalmologist also considers:

- Eye pressure
- Optic nerve appearance
- OCT scans
- Visual fields
- Family history
- Age
- Previous eye pressure measurements
- Rate of progression

No single measurement determines whether you have glaucoma.

---

# Does Corneal Thickness Change Over Time?

In most people, central corneal thickness remains relatively stable throughout adult life.

Routine repeat pachymetry is usually unnecessary unless there is a specific clinical reason.

---

# Is Pachymetry Needed for Everyone?

Not always.

However, it is strongly recommended for many people who have:

- Ocular hypertension
- Suspected glaucoma
- Established glaucoma
- Uncertain eye pressure measurements

Knowing corneal thickness helps your ophthalmologist interpret other test results more accurately.

---

# Can Contact Lenses Affect Pachymetry?

Temporary changes may occur after prolonged contact lens wear.

Your ophthalmologist will advise you if lenses should be removed before testing.

---

# Is Pachymetry Safe?

Yes.

Pachymetry is a safe, quick and routinely performed investigation.

Complications are extremely uncommon.

---

# Questions to Ask About Your Pachymetry

You may wish to ask:

- Is my cornea thinner or thicker than average?
- Does this affect how you interpret my eye pressure?
- Does it change my glaucoma risk?
- Does it alter my treatment plan?

Understanding the answers is often more useful than remembering the exact number.

---

# My Philosophy

Pachymetry is a small test with an important role.

It reminds us that glaucoma care is never about one number.

A pressure reading without corneal thickness can be misleading.

Equally, corneal thickness alone never diagnoses glaucoma.

Every result must be interpreted alongside your examination, imaging and long-term follow-up.

The goal is not simply to measure your cornea.

The goal is to understand what that measurement means for **your** eyes.

---

# Frequently Asked Questions

## Is pachymetry painful?

No.

The test is quick and painless.

---

## Do I need eye drops before the test?

For ultrasound pachymetry, anaesthetic eye drops are usually used.

Optical pachymetry is non-contact and generally does not require anaesthetic drops.

---

## Does a thin cornea mean I need treatment?

Not by itself.

Treatment decisions depend on your overall glaucoma risk.

---

## Will my corneal thickness change?

In most healthy adults, it remains relatively stable.

---

## Should pachymetry be repeated every year?

Usually not.

Your ophthalmologist will advise if repeat testing is needed.

---

# Key Take-Home Messages

- Pachymetry measures the thickness of the cornea.
- Corneal thickness influences how eye pressure is interpreted.
- A thin cornea does not mean you have glaucoma.
- A thick cornea does not protect against glaucoma.
- Pachymetry is one important part of a comprehensive glaucoma assessment.
