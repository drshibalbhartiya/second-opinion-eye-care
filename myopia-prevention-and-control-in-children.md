# Myopia Prevention and Control in Children

> **Good childhood eye care is not just about prescribing glasses. It is about protecting vision for a lifetime.**

Childhood myopia (short-sightedness) is becoming increasingly common around the world.

More children are spending long hours indoors, studying, and using digital devices. While genetics play an important role, lifestyle also influences how quickly myopia develops and progresses.

Fortunately, there is good news.

Modern research shows that we can often slow the progression of myopia and reduce the risk of future eye disease.

This guide explains what parents need to know, what works, what doesn't, and how to give children the best chance of maintaining healthy vision throughout life.

---

# What is Myopia?

Myopia, also known as **short-sightedness**, means that distant objects appear blurry while nearby objects are seen clearly.

In most children, this happens because the eye grows slightly longer than normal.

Light entering the eye focuses in front of the retina instead of directly on it, causing blurred distance vision.

As children continue to grow, myopia may increase.

---

# Why Does Childhood Myopia Matter?

Many parents think myopia simply means needing stronger glasses every year.

It is much more important than that.

Higher degrees of myopia increase the lifetime risk of:

- Retinal detachment
- Myopic macular degeneration
- Glaucoma
- Cataract
- Permanent vision loss

Even slowing myopia by one or two dioptres can significantly reduce these future risks.

Myopia control is an investment in lifelong eye health.

---

# Why is Myopia Becoming More Common?

Several factors contribute.

## Genetics

Children with one myopic parent are more likely to become myopic.

The risk is even higher when both parents are myopic.

However, genes are not destiny.

Lifestyle still matters.

---

## Less Time Outdoors

Children today spend far less time outdoors than previous generations.

Natural daylight appears to protect against the development of myopia.

Experts generally recommend **at least two hours of outdoor time every day** whenever possible.

Outdoor play is one of the simplest and most effective ways to support healthy visual development.

---

## Increased Near Work

Reading is healthy.

Learning is important.

The problem is not reading itself.

The problem is prolonged near work without breaks.

Examples include:

- homework
- tablets
- smartphones
- computers
- gaming
- prolonged studying

Regular breaks reduce visual fatigue and encourage healthier viewing habits.

---

# Signs Your Child May Have Myopia

Watch for:

- Squinting to see distant objects
- Sitting very close to the television
- Holding books very close
- Difficulty seeing the classroom board
- Frequent headaches
- Eye rubbing
- Reduced interest in outdoor sports
- Complaints of blurred distance vision

If you notice these signs, arrange a comprehensive eye examination.

---

# When Should Children Have Eye Examinations?

A child should have an eye examination:

- Before starting school
- Whenever vision problems are suspected
- If there is a strong family history of myopia
- Every 1–2 years during school age, or more frequently if advised
- More often if myopia is progressing

Children often do not realise their vision is poor because they assume everyone sees the world the same way.

# Can Vitamins Prevent Myopia?

No.

This is an important misconception.

No vitamin, supplement or "eye tonic" has been proven to prevent or slow the progression of childhood myopia.

A healthy diet supports healthy eyes.

It does not replace evidence-based myopia control.

---

# Screen Time and Children's Eyes

Digital devices do not directly cause myopia by themselves.

However, excessive screen use often replaces outdoor activity and encourages prolonged near work.

Both of these are associated with a higher risk of myopia progression.

The goal is not to eliminate screens.

The goal is to use them wisely.

---

# Healthy Screen Habits

Encourage children to:

- Hold screens at least 40 cm away.
- Avoid using phones while lying down.
- Use good lighting.
- Blink regularly.
- Take frequent breaks.
- Avoid screens immediately before bedtime.

---

# The 20-20-20 Rule

Every 20 minutes:

Look at something approximately 20 feet away.

For at least 20 seconds.

This relaxes focusing effort and reduces digital eye strain.

---

# Outdoor Time

Outdoor activity is one of the strongest lifestyle factors known to reduce the risk of developing myopia.

Aim for:

**At least two hours outdoors every day.**

Outdoor play may include:

- walking
- cycling
- sports
- playground activities
- gardening
- family outings

The important factor is exposure to natural daylight.

---

# Reading Habits

Healthy reading habits include:

- Reading in good lighting.
- Maintaining a comfortable working distance (30–40 cm).
- Sitting upright.
- Taking regular breaks.
- Avoiding prolonged uninterrupted near work.

Reading itself is beneficial.

Healthy reading habits are even better.

---

# Sleep

Adequate sleep supports healthy growth and overall wellbeing.

Although sleep alone does not prevent myopia, healthy daily routines often include:

- regular sleep schedules
- physical activity
- outdoor play
- balanced nutrition

Together these support healthy visual development.

---

# Glasses

Children should wear the glasses prescribed for them.

Undercorrecting myopia is **not recommended** and does not slow progression.

Good vision supports learning, confidence and safety.

---

# Myopia Control Treatments

Modern eye care offers several evidence-based methods to slow myopia progression.

Not every child needs every treatment.

Recommendations depend on:

- age
- degree of myopia
- rate of progression
- family history
- lifestyle
- examination findings

---

## Low-Dose Atropine

Low-dose atropine eye drops may slow myopia progression in many children.

Treatment is individualised.

Regular follow-up is essential.

---

## Myopia-Control Spectacle Lenses

Special spectacle lenses can slow the progression of myopia while providing clear vision.

They are different from ordinary glasses.

---

## Multifocal Contact Lenses

Selected children may benefit from specially designed soft contact lenses that help slow eye growth.

Good hygiene and careful supervision are essential.

---

## Orthokeratology

Orthokeratology uses specially designed rigid contact lenses worn overnight to temporarily reshape the cornea.

This treatment requires careful patient selection and regular monitoring.

---

# Monitoring Progression

Managing myopia is not just about prescribing glasses.

Regular monitoring helps determine whether treatment is working.

Follow-up may include:

- vision testing
- refraction
- axial length measurements (when available)
- assessment of progression
- discussion of lifestyle habits

---

# Common Myths

## "Reading causes myopia."

Reading does not cause myopia by itself.

Long periods of uninterrupted near work may contribute.

---

## "Carrots prevent myopia."

Carrots are healthy.

They do not prevent or cure myopia.

---

## "Glasses make eyesight weaker."

False.

Glasses improve vision.

They do not make the eyes weaker.

---

## "Children will outgrow myopia."

Most children do not.

Myopia usually progresses while the eyes are still growing.

---

## "Less screen time alone will stop myopia."

Reducing excessive screen time is helpful.

It should be combined with outdoor activity, regular eye examinations and, when appropriate, evidence-based myopia control treatments.

---

# A Healthy Daily Routine

### Morning

- Wear prescribed glasses.
- Eat a healthy breakfast.
- Walk to school if possible.

### During School

- Sit where the board is clearly visible.
- Maintain good reading posture.
- Take visual breaks.

### After School

- Spend at least two hours outdoors.
- Encourage active play.

### Homework

- Good lighting.
- Comfortable working distance.
- Follow the 20-20-20 rule.

### Evening

- Reduce recreational screen time before bed.
- Get adequate sleep.

---

# When Should Parents Seek Medical Advice?

Arrange an eye examination if your child:

- cannot see the classroom board
- squints frequently
- complains of blurred vision
- has rapidly increasing spectacle power
- has headaches related to vision
- has a strong family history of high myopia

Early intervention often provides the greatest benefit.

---

# My Philosophy

As an ophthalmologist, I believe that childhood myopia care should be proactive rather than reactive.

The goal is not simply to prescribe stronger glasses every year.

The goal is to reduce the lifetime risk of eye disease.

Treatment should combine:

- regular monitoring
- evidence-based myopia control
- healthy lifestyle habits
- parent education
- partnership with the family

Every child is different.

Treatment should always be individualised.

# Frequently Asked Questions

## Can myopia be cured?

No.

It can usually be corrected with glasses or contact lenses, and its progression can often be slowed.

---

## Do all children need atropine?

No.

Treatment should be individualised.

---

## How much outdoor time is recommended?

Aim for at least two hours every day whenever possible.

---

## Does eating carrots improve eyesight?

Carrots are nutritious but do not prevent or cure myopia.

---

## Can myopia stop increasing?

Yes.

Progression usually slows in late adolescence, although the timing varies from child to child.

---

# Key Take-Home Messages

- Myopia is becoming increasingly common.
- Early detection is important.
- Outdoor time protects children's eyes.
- Healthy screen habits matter.
- Good nutrition supports overall eye health.
- No vitamin can prevent myopia.
- Several treatments can slow progression.
- Regular follow-up allows timely adjustments.
- The goal is lifelong healthy vision, not simply stronger glasses.
