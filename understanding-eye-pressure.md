# Understanding Eye Pressure (Intraocular Pressure)

> **Eye pressure is one of the most important measurements in glaucoma—but it is not the same as glaucoma.**

Many patients become understandably anxious when they hear that their eye pressure is "high."

Others are confused when they develop glaucoma despite having "normal" eye pressure.

The truth is that eye pressure is only one piece of the puzzle.

Understanding what it means—and what it does not mean—can help you make better decisions about your eye health.

---

# What Is Eye Pressure?

Eye pressure, also called **intraocular pressure (IOP)**, is the pressure created by the fluid inside the eye.

Your eye constantly:

- Produces fluid (aqueous humour)
- Circulates the fluid
- Drains the fluid away

When production and drainage are balanced, eye pressure remains stable.

---

# Why Does Eye Pressure Matter?

The optic nerve is sensitive to pressure.

For some people, pressure that is too high may damage the optic nerve over time, leading to glaucoma.

However:

- Not everyone with high pressure develops glaucoma.
- Not everyone with glaucoma has high pressure.

This is why eye pressure is a **risk factor**, not a diagnosis.

---

# What Is a Normal Eye Pressure?

Most healthy adults have an eye pressure between **10 and 21 mmHg**.

However, there is no "perfect" number.

For example:

- Some people develop glaucoma at 15 mmHg.
- Others never develop glaucoma despite pressures of 24 mmHg.

The right pressure depends on **your** optic nerve.

---

# Does High Eye Pressure Mean I Have Glaucoma?

No.

High eye pressure alone is called **ocular hypertension**.

Some people with ocular hypertension never develop optic nerve damage.

Others eventually do.

Your ophthalmologist assesses:

- Optic nerve appearance
- OCT scans
- Visual fields
- Corneal thickness
- Family history
- Age
- Overall risk

before deciding whether treatment is needed.

---

# Can You Have Glaucoma With Normal Eye Pressure?

Yes.

This is called **normal-tension glaucoma**.

In these patients, the optic nerve is damaged even though eye pressure remains within the traditionally "normal" range.

This is one reason why eye pressure alone cannot diagnose or exclude glaucoma.

---

# Why Does My Eye Pressure Change?

Eye pressure naturally changes throughout the day.

Small fluctuations are normal.

Pressure may also vary because of:

- Time of day
- Body position
- Stress
- Measurement technique
- Corneal thickness
- Certain medications
- Previous eye surgery

One isolated reading rarely tells the whole story.

---

# Why Is My Eye Pressure Different Every Visit?

Many patients notice that today's pressure differs from last month's.

This is common.

Small differences do not necessarily mean your glaucoma is worsening.

Your ophthalmologist looks for:

- Long-term trends
- The range of pressure over time
- Whether the pressure is appropriate for your optic nerve

rather than focusing on one reading.

---

# What Is Target Eye Pressure?

Target eye pressure is the pressure your ophthalmologist believes is most likely to protect your optic nerve from further damage.

It is **not** the same for everyone.

Target pressure depends on:

- Severity of glaucoma
- Rate of progression
- Age
- Life expectancy
- Previous damage
- Risk factors

As glaucoma changes, your target pressure may also change.

---

# How Is Eye Pressure Measured?

Several methods are available.

The most accurate routine method is **Goldmann applanation tonometry**.

Other methods include:

- Non-contact ("air puff") tonometry
- Rebound tonometry
- Handheld tonometers

Different instruments may produce slightly different readings.

---

# Does Corneal Thickness Matter?

Yes.

A thin cornea may make pressure appear lower than it really is.

A thick cornea may make pressure appear higher.

This is why many glaucoma patients undergo **pachymetry**.

Corneal thickness helps your ophthalmologist interpret eye pressure more accurately.

---

# Can Eye Pressure Be Too Low?

Yes.

Very low eye pressure is uncommon but may occur after:

- Eye surgery
- Eye injury
- Certain inflammatory conditions

Persistently low pressure may also affect vision and requires medical assessment.

---

# Can Stress Increase Eye Pressure?

Stress does not usually cause large or sustained increases in eye pressure.

However, anxiety during an examination may influence measurements in some people.

Long-term glaucoma management should never be based on a single reading.

---

# Does Exercise Affect Eye Pressure?

Regular exercise has many health benefits and may produce a modest reduction in eye pressure in some people.

However:

- Exercise is **not** a substitute for glaucoma treatment.
- Certain activities may temporarily increase eye pressure.

Discuss specific exercise recommendations with your ophthalmologist if you have advanced glaucoma.

---

# Can Food or Vitamins Lower Eye Pressure?

No specific food or supplement has been proven to replace glaucoma treatment.

A healthy diet supports overall health but should not be considered a treatment for elevated eye pressure.

---

# Should I Check My Eye Pressure at Home?

Home tonometry may be useful for selected patients.

However, most people do not require home monitoring.

Your ophthalmologist will advise if it is appropriate in your situation.

---

# My Philosophy

Eye pressure is one of the most important numbers in glaucoma—but it is never the only number that matters.

I never make treatment decisions based solely on today's pressure.

Instead, I consider:

- Eye pressure
- Optic nerve appearance
- OCT scans
- Visual fields
- Corneal thickness
- Previous examinations
- Risk of progression
- Your quality of life

The goal is not to achieve the lowest possible pressure.

The goal is to preserve your vision with the least possible treatment burden.

---

# Frequently Asked Questions

## What is the ideal eye pressure?

There is no universal ideal pressure.

The safest pressure depends on your optic nerve and your individual risk.

---

## Does a pressure of 22 mmHg mean I have glaucoma?

No.

It simply means further assessment may be appropriate.

---

## Can glaucoma develop if my pressure is normal?

Yes.

Normal-tension glaucoma is well recognised.

---

## Why is my pressure different every visit?

Small fluctuations are common and usually expected.

---

## Should I worry about one high reading?

Usually not.

Your ophthalmologist interprets pressure measurements over time rather than relying on a single result.

---

# Key Take-Home Messages

- Eye pressure is an important risk factor for glaucoma—but it is not glaucoma itself.
- High pressure does not always cause glaucoma.
- Normal pressure does not always exclude glaucoma.
- Pressure naturally fluctuates.
- Treatment decisions should always consider the complete clinical picture.
